> Method note for the report writer (read first): research date 30 Oct 2026. ## 1. AI and automation risk (accessibility testing/remediation and AI website builders), 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Automation now finds and fixes the easy, high-volume part of WCAG failures (contrast, alt text, form labels: the bulk of detectable errors). That part is turning into a commodity: overlays at about $40–50/month, Fiverr gigs from about $50, and free self-check tools, including one run by Latvia's own regulator. Human-verified conformance work (keyboard, focus, reading order, app flows) is still not automatable, and it is exactly the part a non-technical reseller cannot quality-check. AI site builders at about $3–$39/month, plus Lithuanian freelancers building WooCommerce shops for €200–850, squeeze the "website + care plan" leg hard.

### Cited Findings
**How much of an audit can be automated**
- Deque's own coverage study says automated testing (axe-core rules) fully covered on average **57%** of accessibility issues by issue volume. This is an older study (2021) and vendor-produced. — [Deque blog](https://www.deque.com/blog/automated-testing-study-identifies-57-percent-of-digital-accessibility-issues/); [Deque Coverage Report PDF](https://accessibility.deque.com/hubfs/Accessibility-Coverage-Report.pdf)
- Deque markets that its tooling, combining automated tests with "Intelligent Guided Tests" (semi-automated with a human answering prompts), finds **up to ~80%** of issues. Vendor claim; not AI-only. — [Deque axe DevTools](https://www.deque.com/axe/devtools/)
- Deque's 2025–2026 AI features: Axe Assistant (a chatbot) and an Axe MCP Server that surfaces suggested code remediations inside IDEs and AI coding agents. Deque frames the AI as supporting human decisions rather than replacing them. — [Deque axe AI](https://www.deque.com/axe/ai/)
- Independent/older benchmark: a UK Government Digital Service test of 13 automated tools is reported to show axe catching about **29%** of known barriers, with every tool missing reading order, missing visible focus and context-dependent colour use. This is 2017 data, seen only via secondary summaries. — [GDS accessibility blog (2017)](https://accessibility.blog.gov.uk/2017/02/24/what-we-found-when-we-tested-tools-on-the-worlds-least-accessible-webpage/); secondary: [Easy A11y Guide](https://easya11yguide.com/tips/how-much-can-automated-accessibility-tools-do/)
- A January 2026 Evinced (vendor) study against 35 manual audits reportedly found Evinced detected **62.8%** of manually found issues versus **22.6%** for axe-core alone. Vendor study, seen via a secondary blog; treat with caution. — [TruthBytes](https://truthbytesback.com/what-ai-accessibility-scanners-actually-catch/)
- Across studies, automated coverage ranges from roughly 20% to 60%+ depending on method (issue volume vs. WCAG criteria). No independent study isolates what newer AI features add. — [Accessibility.Works](https://www.accessibility.works/blog/automated-wcag-testing-tools-accessibility-compliance/); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/chris_devto/your-accessibility-score-is-lying-to-you-5fh2)

**How much remediation can be automated**
- LLM-based remediation is now a research field. An arXiv paper (2602.17887, 2026) presents a tool that uses axe-core to detect issues and the OpenAI API to generate corrected HTML, and notes single-page apps are harder than static sites. — [arXiv 2602.17887](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17887)
- Vendors now sell source-code AI remediation: an AI agent edits the repo or theme, opens a pull request, and a human reviews it. A vendor claims AI "can fix most common WCAG issues" with human review. Vendor marketing. — [TestParty: Can AI remediate?](https://testparty.ai/blog/can-ai-remediate-website-accessibility); [TestParty: remediation tools 2026](https://testparty.ai/blog/accessibility-remediation-tools-2026)
- AudioEye markets "AI automation + expert audits + custom code fixes" and claims about **97%** of issues resolved. Its fixes run as JavaScript on the live page (overlay-adjacent). — [AudioEye platform](https://www.audioeye.com/audioeye-digital-accessibility-platform/)
- Shopify app roundups (2026) list accessiBe, AudioEye, Accessibility Assistant and TestParty as Shopify accessibility apps that a merchant can install directly. — [easyappsecom Shopify roundup](https://easyappsecom.com/guides/best-shopify-accessibility-apps)

**What the errors actually are (why the cheap end commoditises)**
- WebAIM Million 2026: **95.9%** of the top 1M homepages had detectable WCAG 2 failures, up from 94.8% in 2025 (the first regression in six years). Average errors per page rose 10.1% to **56.1**, and average page elements rose 22.5% to 1,437. Secondary coverage; the primary report was not opened. — [Priority Pixels](https://prioritypixels.co.uk/insights/web-accessibility-is-getting-worse-according-to-the-2026-webaim-million-report/); [Matthias Ott](https://matthiasott.com/notes/this-still-not-for-everyone); [Nexer Digital](https://www.nexerdigital.com/news-and-thoughts/the-webaim-million-report-2026-what-it-tells-us)
- Most common failures in 2026: low-contrast text (**83.9%** of homepages), missing alt text (**53.1%**), missing form labels (**51.0%**). Six failure types account for about **96%** of detected errors. — [Priority Pixels](https://prioritypixels.co.uk/insights/web-accessibility-is-getting-worse-according-to-the-2026-webaim-million-report/); [TestParty WCAG violations data](https://testparty.ai/blog/wcag-violations-data)

### Inferences
- **[Judgment]** The audit-plus-fix-plus-monitoring offer is being squeezed from both sides. At the bottom, automated scanning, LLM fixes, $40/month overlays and free regulator tools take the easy 30–60% of the work. At the top, the remaining human-verified work (full conformance, mobile apps, assistive-technology testing) needs credible specialists. A middleman who resells Indian scan reports sits in the most commoditised part of the value chain.
- **[Judgment]** "Monitoring" as a recurring revenue line is especially weak. Scheduled automated scans are a standard feature of $40/month tools, so a reseller monthly fee competes against an SME simply buying the tool.
- **[Judgment]** The "WordPress/Shopify websites with care plans" leg competes with €130–850 local freelancers and $3–39/month AI builders. With an offshore delivery layer plus a reseller margin, it is hard to price below local Lithuanian/Estonian freelancers while also adding the language and support advantage a Baltic buyer expects.
- **[Estimate]** Because six failure types make up about 96% of detected errors, a non-specialist SME can plausibly fix a large share of flagged issues with an AI assistant or a plugin. That shrinks the paid "fix" market to the hard residue.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 independent (non-vendor) benchmark of AI-specific detection or remediation accuracy was found.
- Pricing for Siteimprove, Silktide, Level Access platform, Deque axe Monitor and Equalize Digital (WordPress) was not found in search results; the Equalize pricing page exists but figures were not captured ([Equalize pricing](https://equalizedigital.com/accessibility-checker/pricing/)).
- Microsoft and Google accessibility tooling (e.g., Lighthouse, Accessibility Insights) and Shopify Magic were not researched in this session.
- No source quantified price declines over time for Baltic website builds; only 2026 snapshots were found.

## 2. Overlay controversy: FTC v. accessiBe, Overlay Fact Sheet, overlay-user lawsuits, European disability organisations

### Takeaway
Relying on overlays is a legal and reputational trap. The FTC finalised a $1M order against accessiBe in April 2025 over claims that its widget makes sites WCAG-compliant. Sites with overlays are still sued in large numbers in the US (about 1,400 in 2025 by one count). The European Disability Forum says overlays do not make a site compliant with European law. A reseller who sells "EAA compliance" via overlays sells the exact claim regulators have punished.

### Cited Findings
- FTC approved the final order against accessiBe on **22 April 2025** by a **3–0** vote. It requires a **$1 million** payment and bars misleading claims that its AI tool (accessWidget) can make any website WCAG-compliant. The case also covered paid reviews presented as independent. The $1M is described as consumer redress, not a civil penalty. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-approves-final-order-requiring-accessibe-pay-1-million); [TechCrunch, Jan 2025 (proposed order)](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/03/ftc-orders-ai-accessibility-startup-accessibe-to-pay-1m-for-misleading-advertising); [Data Privacy + Security Insider](https://www.dataprivacyandsecurityinsider.com/2025/04/ftc-settles-with-accessibe-for-misleading-statements-about-wcag-compliance/)
- Silktide reports that **1,416** sites/companies using overlays were sued in 2025. Secondary; based on lawsuit trackers. — [Silktide](https://silktide.com/s/overlays-got-1416-sites-sued-in-2025/)
- Overlay share of US digital accessibility lawsuits: one source says **28%** in 2025 (up from 25% in 2024); another says **22.6%** in H1 2025. These conflict, and both are secondary attributions to UsableNet data. — [TestParty overlay lawsuit data](https://testparty.ai/blog/accessibility-overlay-lawsuits-data); [Accessibility.Works](https://www.accessibility.works/blog/accessibility-overlay-widgets-attract-lawsuits/)
- UsableNet's midyear report counted **2,019** US digital accessibility filings in H1 2025. A secondary site attributes a full-year 2025 figure of **4,928** to UsableNet's year-end report (federal and state combined). Not verified at usablenet.com. — [Newswire: lawsuits surge 2025](https://www.newswire.com/news/digital-accessibility-lawsuits-surge-in-2025-22618502); [TestParty](https://testparty.ai/blog/accessibility-overlay-lawsuits-data)
- Seyfarth Shaw (primary law-firm count): **3,117** federal ADA Title III website accessibility lawsuits in 2025, up 27% from 2,452 in 2024 and the highest since 2022. Website cases were 36% of 8,667 total Title III federal filings. — [Seyfarth / adatitleiii.com, Mar 2026](https://www.adatitleiii.com/2026/03/federal-court-website-accessibility-lawsuit-filings-bounce-back-in-2025/); [Mondaq: 8,667 Title III filings](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/constitutional-administrative-law/1745806/ada-title-iii-federal-lawsuit-filings-fall-slightly-to-8667-in-2025)
- EDF + IAAP joint statement (May 2023; older but still the standing position): overlays "do not make the website accessible or compliant with European accessibility legislation" and are not an acceptable substitute for fixing the site. It also raises privacy concerns (detecting assistive-technology use can reveal disability status) and notes that overlays duplicate built-in browser/OS features and can interfere with assistive technology. — [EDF: overlays don't guarantee compliance](https://www.edf-feph.org/accessibility-overlays-dont-guarantee-compliance-with-european-legislation/); [EDF joint statement](https://www.edf-feph.org/publications/joint-statement-on-accessibility-overlays/)
- The Overlay Fact Sheet (maintained by Karl Groves) reportedly has **>1,000** signatures from accessibility practitioners as of Sep 2026 (vendor-affiliated blog), up from 233 in 2021. The live count was not checked. — [TestParty overlay problems](https://testparty.ai/blog/accessibility-overlay-problems); [Karl Groves 2021](https://karlgroves.com/?p=2390)
- Forrester, commenting on Level Access buying UserWay: overlays take a "bolt-on" approach, and Level Access would need to market the technology ethically. — [Forrester blog](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/digital-accessibility-vendor-level-access-to-acquire-userway-in-a-first-of-its-kind-deal/)
- The French Carrefour ruling (see Q3) reportedly rejected a partial compliance score (71% on RGAA criteria) as a defence and demanded full accessibility. A widget that "improves" a score would not meet this standard. Secondary reporting. — [Atyantik enforcement summary](https://atyantik.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-enforcement/); [li.solutions](https://li.solutions/blog/eaa-enforcement-2026/)

### Inferences
- **[Judgment]** If the business resells overlays (directly, or via a white-label supplier that quietly installs one), it carries three risks: (a) its clients stay exposed to complaints and lawsuits, (b) it repeats the claim pattern the FTC sanctioned, and (c) it is publicly opposed by the EU's main disability umbrella body. Any Baltic disability organisation or competitor could use this against the seller.
- **[Judgment]** The cheapest Indian white-label "EAA packages" are likely to include overlays or automated-scan-only reports, because that is the only way to hit low price points. The founder would need contractual bans on overlays and the technical ability to verify the ban, which he lacks.
- **[Judgment]** US lawsuit numbers matter mainly as evidence that "overlay = protection" is false. They do not show Baltic demand, because the EU has no comparable private damages-litigation machine (see Q3).

### Gaps
- UsableNet's 2025 year-end report was not opened. The overlay share (22.6% vs 28%) and whether 1,416/1,400+ counts companies or suits remain unresolved.
- No EU (let alone Baltic) case specifically targeting an overlay user under the EAA was found.
- No statement from Estonian, Latvian or Lithuanian disability organisations on overlays was found.
- The FTC order's duration (20 years per secondary sources) was not verified.

## 3. Enforcement reality since 28 June 2025, and whether weak enforcement destroys demand

### Takeaway
Enforcement is real but concentrated in France (association-led injunctions, plus a June 2026 court order against Carrefour), the Netherlands (ACM audits), Sweden (PTS proactive cases), Germany (a market-surveillance authority active since Jan 2026, plus contested warning-letter waves) and Ireland (ComReg complaints). No EU-wide confirmed EAA fine was found. In the Baltics, the regulators (TTJA in Estonia, PTAC in Latvia, VVTAT in Lithuania) are visible mainly through reminders, campaigns and free self-assessment tools. No published Baltic accessibility fines or inspection statistics were found. Most Baltic SMEs are microenterprises, which are exempt for services. Comparable laws show demand spikes around deadlines and then decays without enforcement or litigation pressure.

### Cited Findings
**France**
- Disability groups ApiDV and Droit Pluriel (backed by the collective Intérêt à Agir) sent formal notices in July 2025 with a 1 Sep 2025 deadline, then filed emergency injunctions in November 2025 against **Auchan, Carrefour, E.Leclerc and Picard**. Secondary (vendor blogs). — [Eye-Able](https://eye-able.com/en/blog/supermarket-accessibility-lawsuit-france-2025); [TestParty France lawsuits](https://testparty.ai/blog/first-european-accessibility-act-lawsuits-france-2025); [getWCAG](https://getwcag.com/en/blog/france-first-eaa-european-accessibility-act-lawsuit)
- A court in Caen ruled on **4 June 2026** that Carrefour France must make its e-commerce site and mobile app fully accessible within **six months**, with a penalty of **€500 per day** of delay. It reportedly rejected a 71% RGAA score as a defence. This is reported as the first national-EAA-law ruling against a retailer. Secondary; the judgment was not seen. Outcomes for Auchan, E.Leclerc and Picard were not found. — [li.solutions](https://li.solutions/blog/eaa-enforcement-2026/); [Atyantik](https://atyantik.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-enforcement/); [Level Access EAA overview](https://www.levelaccess.com/compliance-overview/european-accessibility-act-eaa/)

**Germany (BFSG)**
- The joint state authority MLBF (Magdeburg) adopted its surveillance strategies on **29 Jan 2026**. It acts reactively on complaints, prioritising high-reach offers, and proactively via partly automated sampling. Fines of up to **€100,000** are reported. — [ITMR Legal](https://itmr-legal.de/blog/bfsg); [Zentrum Barrierefreiheit](https://zentrum-barrierefreiheit.de/marktueberwachung-schlichtung/)
- Two private warning-letter (Abmahnung) waves are reported since summer 2025, the second since Feb 2026 with demands of about **€2,700**. Other German sources say BFSG warning letters rest on § 3a UWG and that this legal basis is not yet settled by courts, with first rulings expected in H2 2026. Sources conflict; mostly agency blogs. — [die-mainagentur](https://die-mainagentur.de/blog/bfsg-2026-zweite-abmahnwelle/); [7aufeinenstreich](https://www.7aufeinenstreich.com/blog/bfsg-bussgeld-abmahnung); [die-mit-ausrufezeichen](https://die-mit-ausrufezeichen.de/bfsg-abmahnung-barrierefreiheit-website/); [creativecouch](https://creativecouch.de/webdesign-wordpress/bfsg-2026-abmahnwellen-und-was-du-jetzt-wissen-musst/)

**Netherlands, Sweden, Ireland**
- Netherlands: ACM ran a mandatory self-reporting cycle that closed in October 2025, with audits of non-reporters prioritised for spring 2026. An ACM check in March 2026 reportedly found **61%** of the largest Dutch webshops tested could not complete an order with assistive technology. — [Atyantik](https://atyantik.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-enforcement/); [Allyant](https://allyant.com/blog/eaa-enforcement-news/); [DevAlly](https://devally.com/blog/What-product-teams-need-to-know-about-eaa)
- Sweden: PTS opened its first e-commerce accessibility cases in **October 2025**, with **28** cases reported by March 2026. Complaints are reported as 124 (Level Access) or 114 for 2025 (Complyloft). — [Atyantik](https://atyantik.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-enforcement/); [Level Access 2026 enforcement blog](https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/eaa-compliance-in-2026-how-enforcement-has-evolved-and-what-to-expect-next/); [Complyloft tracker](https://www.complyloft.com/accessibility/eaa/enforcement)
- Ireland: ComReg is handling complaints, including one against the operator Three. Ireland is described as the only member state with criminal sanctions; the reported prison term conflicts across sources (6 vs 18 months). — [Auditsu](https://auditsu.com/resources/eaa-enforcement-2026); [web-accessibility-checker](https://web-accessibility-checker.com/en/blog/eaa-fines-penalties-by-country)
- Trackers report **no confirmed EU fine under the EAA** as of their latest updates; enforcement is mostly complaint- and litigation-driven, with Sweden the exception. — [Level Access 2026 enforcement blog](https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/eaa-compliance-in-2026-how-enforcement-has-evolved-and-what-to-expect-next/); [Complyloft](https://www.complyloft.com/accessibility/eaa/enforcement)

**Comparable laws: how demand behaved**
- US ADA: demand is driven by private plaintiff litigation; there were 3,117 federal website suits in 2025, concentrated in NY (1,021), FL (961) and IL (585). — [Seyfarth / adatitleiii.com](https://www.adatitleiii.com/2026/03/federal-court-website-accessibility-lawsuit-filings-bounce-back-in-2025/)
- GDPR (2018): pre-deadline boom ("everyone is claiming now to be a GDPR expert"), with French consultant job postings jumping sharply in April 2018. ## 5. Reseller / white-label model risks

### Takeaway
The documented risks of reselling offshore work (quality control loss, communication lag, scope creep, hidden rework costs, supplier lock-in or disappearance, client poaching) are all amplified in compliance work. Here the deliverable is a legal claim ("your site meets EAA"), courts demand full accessibility, and the seller cannot technically verify what the supplier delivers. Evidence on this is mostly from vendor blogs; no independent churn or margin data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Recurring white-label risks: lost quality control, communication and timeline misalignment, client poaching and IP leakage, data and security exposure, hidden costs eroding margin, vendor lock-in or provider failure. The source is a white-label vendor. — [BSS Commerce](https://bsscommerce.com/services/blog/white-label-development-risks/)
- Failures most often come from workflow (unclear scope, inconsistent QA, messy feedback loops, pricing that doesn't match real delivery effort) more than from vendor skill. — [Geeks for Growth: agency mistakes](https://geeksforgrowth.com/blog/white-label-agency-mistakes/); [Geeks for Growth: margins](https://geeksforgrowth.com/white-label-agency-margins/)
- Vendor claim: agency margins of 30–40% on domestic staff vs 60–75% with white-label offshore teams. Unverified marketing. — [VA Masters](https://vamasters.com/white-label-outsourcing-agencies-offshore-teams/)
- Hidden offshore costs (management overhead, tooling, delay-driven rework) erode margins; communication barriers and misaligned expectations on timelines and scope. — [DEV Community / Zoolatech](https://dev.to/zoolatech/the-hidden-challenges-of-offshore-development-and-how-to-overcome-them-7nm)
- Responsiveness gaps (24–48h replies) drive client churn, because clients expect faster answers. Competitor-style source. — [Boundev](https://www.boundev.ai/blog/when-to-switch-white-label-agency-partners); [Growthable](https://growthable.io/blogs/reducing-churn-rates-professional-whitelabel-support-services/)
- Liability context: the Carrefour order required full accessibility within six months at €500/day. German warning letters reportedly demand about €2,700. An SME that bought "compliance" from a reseller and then gets a precept, warning letter or injunction will look to the reseller. — [li.solutions](https://li.solutions/blog/eaa-enforcement-2026/); [die-mainagentur](https://die-mainagentur.de/blog/bfsg-2026-zweite-abmahnwelle/)
- Several Fiverr accessibility sellers state they are technical auditors, not lawyers, and that their work is not legal advice. ### Inferences
- **[Judgment]** The core structural flaw: the supplier disclaims compliance, the client buys compliance, and the reseller sits in the middle holding the liability without the expertise to check the work. Professional-indemnity insurance for a one-person reseller making compliance claims may be expensive or unavailable (not researched).
- **[Judgment]** Giving an offshore supplier admin access to client CMSs and e-shops creates GDPR processor and international-transfer questions and security exposure. That is an extra sales objection for regulated buyers such as fintechs (not researched in detail).
- **[Judgment]** Time-zone and language mismatch: Baltic SME clients will ask questions in Lithuanian, Latvian, Estonian or Russian. Each message has to be translated and relayed by the founder, which consumes exactly the 5–10 hours he has.
