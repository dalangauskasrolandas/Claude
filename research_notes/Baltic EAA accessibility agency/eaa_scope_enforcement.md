## 1. National transposition laws (LT, LV, EE), dates, official links, late transposition / infringement

### Takeaway
All three countries have a stand-alone EAA transposition act, and all have applied since 28 June 2025. Estonia adopted its act on time (published June 2022). Lithuania (Dec 2022) and Latvia (March 2023) were late and received EU letters of formal notice in July 2022. Latvia was later sent a reasoned opinion for incomplete transposition. Lithuania substantially amended its law with effect from 1 April 2026, and some amended provisions apply only from 28 June 2027.

## 2. Scope: which services are covered, and does it cover an ordinary SME marketing website or a B2B-only shop?

### Takeaway
For a website or app, the EAA applies only to the listed consumer services:
- e-commerce
- consumer banking
- e-books
- electronic communications
- access to audiovisual media
- the digital elements of air, bus, rail and waterborne passenger transport

"E-commerce services" requires distance and electronic provision, at the individual request of a **consumer**, **with a view to concluding a consumer contract**. On the Directive's wording, a purely informational or marketing website of an ordinary SME (no consumer purchase, booking or contract flow) and a B2B-only shop are **outside scope**. This sharply narrows the addressable market. National authorities' public messaging (LV PTAC, LT VVTAT) frames the target as e-shops and apps selling to consumers.

### Cited Findings
- Covered services (provided to consumers after 28 June 2025), Directive Art. 2(2) — [Directive (EU) 2019/882, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj):
  - (a) electronic communications services (except transmission services used for machine-to-machine services);
  - (b) services providing access to audiovisual media services;
  - (c) elements of air, bus, rail and waterborne passenger transport services: websites, mobile apps, e-tickets and e-ticketing services, transport information including real-time information, and interactive self-service terminals. For urban, suburban and regional transport only the terminals element applies;
  - (d) consumer banking services;
  - (e) e-books and dedicated software;
  - (f) e-commerce services.
  - Art. 2(3) adds answering emergency communications to 112.
  - Covered products (Art. 2(1)): consumer computers and operating systems, payment terminals, ATMs, ticketing and check-in machines, interactive information self-service terminals, consumer smartphones/terminal equipment, TV equipment for digital TV, and e-readers.
- Directive Art. 3(30) defines "e-commerce services" as services provided at a distance, through websites and mobile device-based services, by electronic means and at the individual request of a consumer with a view to concluding a consumer contract — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj). Commentary stresses that these criteria are **cumulative** — [accessible.org](https://accessible.org/eaa-ecommerce-services-requirements/); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/inclusivity-by-design-the-european-accessibility-act-as-a-new-imperative-for-e-102lu76).
- Secondary guides state that brochure/informational sites and shops whose buyers are all businesses generally fall outside the e-commerce definition. A web shop selling to consumers is covered; a purely B2B shop is not — [Going Stellar](https://goingstellar.ro/research/european-accessibility-act-online-stores/); [Inclusely](https://inclusely.com/laws/european-accessibility-act).
- Contrary signal: one guide relies on a recital (cited as Recital 43) saying the e-commerce obligations apply to the online sale of *any* product or service — [dardo.studio](https://dardo.studio/en/blog/european-accessibility-act/). This widens *what is sold* (any product or service), not *who buys*. The consumer-contract element remains.
- Search for official Commission guidance on Art. 3(30) and informational sites returned **no official Commission FAQ/notice** — only secondary sources (search on "e-commerce services definition consumer contract informational website", results above).
- **Latvia (PTAC):**
  - The law sets accessibility requirements for e-commerce services. These cover all e-commerce sites (online shops) and apps offering goods or services — [PTAC news](https://www.ptac.gov.lv/lv/jaunums/ptac-aicina-e-komercijas-uznemumus-nodrosinat-digitalo-pakalpojumu-pieklustamibu); [PTAC: requirements for services](https://www.ptac.gov.lv/lv/pieklustamibas-prasibas-pakalpojumiem).
  - PTAC's 2026 surveillance priority names websites of **e-commerce and insurance services** — [PTAC priorities](https://www.ptac.gov.lv/lv/ptac-prioritates).
- **Lithuania (VVTAT):** digital accessibility requirements apply from 28 June 2025. Online shops are among the covered services. Per the VVTAT summary, the duty applies to "medium and large" companies while very small firms are exempt — [VVTAT reminder](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos-ir-pranesimai-ziniasklaidai-377/vvtat-primena-apie-prieinamumo-reikalavimu-isigaliojima/); [VVTAT general info](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/prieinamumo-reikalavimai/bendra-informacija/). See section 3 for why "medium and large" is probably loose wording.
- **Estonia:** from 28 June 2025 the digital accessibility requirements extend to *certain* private-sector service providers — [TTJA digital accessibility page](https://ttja.ee/eraklient/tarbija-oigused/kaubandus-teenused/digiligipaasetavuse-tagamine). Estonian guides list e-commerce, financial, television and similar services — [UX Estonia](https://uxestonia.ee/blogi/ligipaasetavuse-seadus-mida-e-kaubanduse-finants-televisiooni-jt-teenuste-ja-toodete-pakkuja-teadma-peaks).
- Lithuania: supervision is split by sector. VVTAT covers e-commerce, consumer banking and e-books; other regulators cover telecoms, audiovisual media and passenger transport — [site.lt](https://site.lt/en/blog/european-accessibility-act). RTK issued guidelines under Art. 6(2) of XIV-1633 on accessibility of audiovisual media access services — [RTK guidelines 2025-02-12](https://rtk.lt/uploads/documents/files/teisine-informacija/20250212%20Gair%C4%97s.pdf).
- Directive Art. 2(4) excludes certain web content:
  - pre-recorded time-based media and office files published before 28 June 2025;
  - online maps (if essential information is provided accessibly);
  - third-party content not funded, developed or controlled by the operator;
  - archived content not updated after 28 June 2025.
  - Source: [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj).

### Inferences
- **In scope (likely):** non-micro businesses whose website or app lets **consumers** buy goods or services or conclude a contract online. This includes online shops, online booking or ordering with contract conclusion (hotels, restaurants with ordering, event ticketing, courses sold online, subscriptions), and marketplaces. Also in scope, regardless of whether they "sell" online, are the consumer-facing websites and apps of banks, credit or payment providers offering consumer banking services, telecoms, and air, bus, rail and ferry operators, because these are separate listed sectors.
- **Out of scope (likely):**
  - a pure brochure or marketing site (dentist, law firm, construction company, manufacturer) with contact details and no consumer contract flow;
  - a B2B-only portal or shop (the buyer is not a "consumer" acting outside trade/profession);
  - the websites of micro-enterprise service providers.
- **Grey zones a seller should not over-claim:** "request a quote" or contact forms only; sites that redirect checkout to a third-party platform; mixed B2B/B2C shops (they are in scope for the consumer flow); and booking-only sites with no online payment. The "with a view to concluding a consumer contract" wording arguably covers online booking even when payment happens later. This is my reading, not official guidance.
- Telling SMEs with brochure sites that the EAA legally obliges them to make the site accessible would be inaccurate and risky (misleading-sales exposure). The real legal hook is B2C e-commerce and the listed sectors.

### Gaps
- No official Commission or national authority guidance was found explicitly confirming the treatment of informational sites, "quote request" sites, or mixed B2B/B2C shops in LT/LV/EE.
- The exact national definitions of "e-commerce service" in XIV-1633 (as amended by XV-760), the LV law and the EE act were not read directly.
- Whether Latvia's Art. 22 assigns consumer banking and passenger transport to Latvijas Banka or transport regulators was not confirmed. Searches returned only SPRK and NEPLP.

## 3. Exemptions, disproportionate burden / fundamental alteration, transition periods, and national gold-plating

### Takeaway
All three countries apply the Directive's micro-enterprise exemption for **service providers**: fewer than 10 employees, and turnover or balance sheet ≤ €2 million. Product micro-enterprises are not exempt, only relieved of documentation. Estonia's wording may be *narrower* than the Directive: it reads as both turnover and balance sheet ≤ €2m. Businesses can claim disproportionate burden or fundamental alteration, but they must document it and, under the Directive, inform the authority. Old service contracts may continue until 28 June 2030, and existing self-service terminals until up to 20 years of use (that is, to 2045 at the latest).

### Cited Findings
- **Directive:**
  - Micro-enterprise: fewer than 10 persons and annual turnover ≤ €2m **or** annual balance sheet ≤ €2m (Art. 3 definitions).
  - Micro-enterprises providing services are exempt from the service accessibility requirements and related obligations (Art. 4(5)).
  - Disproportionate burden / fundamental alteration (Art. 14 and Annex VI criteria). The assessment must be documented, kept for 5 years and renewed every 5 years for services. Operators relying on it must inform the relevant authorities, and micro-enterprises are relieved of that information duty.
  - Transitional provisions (Art. 32): service contracts agreed before 28 June 2025 may continue unchanged until expiry but no longer than five years from that date. Products lawfully used before 28 June 2025 to provide services may be used until 28 June 2030. Self-service terminals lawfully used before 28 June 2025 may be used until the end of their economically useful life, but no longer than 20 years after being put into use.
  - Source: [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj). Article numbers are from the official text but were not re-opened this session.
- **Estonia — exact wording (Riigi Teataja):**
  - "Mikroettevõtja käesoleva seaduse tähenduses on ettevõtja, kellel on alla kümne töötaja ja kelle aastakäive ega aastane bilansimaht ei ületa kahte miljonit eurot." (A micro-enterprise is an undertaking with fewer than ten employees and whose annual turnover *nor* annual balance-sheet total exceeds €2m.)
  - "Käesoleva seaduse nõudeid ei kohaldata mikroettevõtjast teenuseosutaja tegevusele." (The Act's requirements do not apply to the activity of a micro-enterprise service provider.)
  - Product micro-enterprises must give TTJA the facts relevant to a disproportionate-burden assessment on request.
  - Sources: [riigiteataja.ee/akt/115062022001](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/115062022001); [Ligipääsuke](http://ligipaasuke.ee/blogi/mida-uut-toob-teenuste-ja-toodete-ligipaasetavuse-seadus-ettevotetele/). An Estonian blog states that "both conditions must be met" for the micro exemption — [veebimajutus.ee / UX Estonia, per search summary](https://www.veebimajutus.ee/blogi/veebilehe-ligipaasetavuse-direktiiv).
- **Estonia:** businesses may claim an exception where compliance would impose a disproportionate burden — [Ligipääsuke](http://ligipaasuke.ee/blogi/mida-uut-toob-teenuste-ja-toodete-ligipaasetavuse-seadus-ettevotetele/).
- **Latvia:** PTAC states that micro-enterprises (fewer than 10 employees and annual turnover or balance sheet ≤ €2m) are exempt from the **service** accessibility requirements. For goods, micro-enterprises are exempt only from documenting the conformity/burden assessment in writing and must supply information when the authority asks — [PTAC accessibility requirements](https://www.ptac.gov.lv/lv/pieklustamibas-prasibas); [EM guidelines](https://em.gov.lv/lv/media/21095/download?attachment=); [TAP annotation](https://tapportals.mk.gov.lv/annotation/0da3f161-4782-4c3a-95b9-3f5f4289e55e).
- **Lithuania:**
  - Service providers that are very small enterprises (*labai mažos įmonės*) are not subject to the requirements — [PwC LT](https://www.pwc.com/lt/lt/apie-mus/naujienos/prieinamumo-reikalavimu-igyvendinimas-versle.html); [VVTAT](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/prieinamumo-reikalavimai/bendra-informacija/).
  - The exemption covers service providers only; manufacturers, importers and distributors of products are not exempt — [Widen](https://widen.legal/news-events/new-law-accessibility-requirements/); [belownil](https://www.belownil.com/en/blog/european-accessibility-act-does-it-apply-to-your-website).
  - Linked and partner enterprises count towards the thresholds — [belownil](https://www.belownil.com/en/blog/european-accessibility-act-does-it-apply-to-your-website).
- **Conflicting LT paraphrase:** one Lithuanian article says the exemption applies to firms with fewer than 10 employees **or** turnover ≤ €2m — [BNS/ebus.lt, per search summary](https://sc.bns.lt/view/item/prieinamumo-direktyva-isigalios-jau-birzeli-imoniu-laukia-naujos-pareigos-ir-baudos-508431). This contradicts the Directive's cumulative test (fewer than 10 employees **and** a financial ceiling). Treat it as a journalistic error unless the e-tar text says otherwise.
- **Lithuania:** Art. 30(2) of XIV-1633 did not enter into force on 28 June 2025 — [e-seimas](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/legalact/TAP/47fd7c60568411edba0ded10be2fa21c/). Certain provisions of amending law XV-760 enter into force on 28 June 2027 — [e-tar XV-760](https://www.e-tar.lt/portal/lt/legalAct/25be17201ed811f1a552c76556910e9c).

### Inferences
- **Estonia — possible narrower micro definition (my legal reading, verify with an Estonian lawyer).** The Estonian "aastakäive *ega* aastane bilansimaht ei ületa" (neither … nor … exceeds) appears to require **both** turnover and balance sheet ≤ €2m. The Directive requires only one of them. So an Estonian firm with fewer than 10 staff, turnover ≤ €2m but balance sheet > €2m (or the reverse) may be **in scope in Estonia** but exempt under the Directive's wording. The practical impact is small: few firms with fewer than 10 staff have more than €2m on either measure.
- **Lithuania — "medium and large" is probably shorthand.** VVTAT's phrase "applies to medium and large companies" is likely loose. Under the Directive and PwC's description only *micro* firms are exempt, so **small enterprises (10–49 staff) are in scope**. Confirm in the consolidated XIV-1633.
- For the client's targeting, the realistic exemption test is: fewer than 10 employees **and** (turnover ≤ €2m or balance sheet ≤ €2m; in Estonia possibly both) → exempt for services. A firm with fewer than 10 employees but over €2m on the relevant measure (e.g., a high-turnover e-shop with few staff) is **in scope**, and these are attractive prospects.
- For services, disproportionate-burden claims require a documented assessment, renewed periodically. Under the Directive the authority must be informed by non-micro firms. Selling "burden assessment documentation" is a defensible service line (inference).

### Gaps
- Exact Lithuanian and Latvian statutory wording of the micro-enterprise definition. The LT law may cross-refer to the Law on Development of SMEs; not verified.
- Whether LT/LV/EE require notification of a disproportionate-burden claim to the authority (and to which authority), with article numbers: not verified from the national texts.
- National transition rules (LT Art. 30(2) content; LV/EE transitional provisions; whether the 2030 contract transition was transposed verbatim): not retrieved.
- Whether any of the three laws goes beyond the Directive (e.g., extending to B2B or the built environment): no evidence found either way, apart from the Estonian wording point above.

## 4. Technical standard and accessibility-information requirements for services

### Takeaway
None of the three laws fixes a single standard in the sources found. In practice, EN 301 549 (v3.2.1, which incorporates WCAG 2.1 Level AA for web content) is the benchmark until EAA-harmonised standards are cited in the Official Journal. Estonia's TTJA explicitly names EN 301 549 V3.2.1. Service providers must publish information on how their service meets the accessibility requirements (Directive Art. 13 and Annex V), usually in terms and conditions or an accessibility statement.

### Cited Findings
- TTJA: the private-sector digital accessibility requirements applicable from 28 June 2025 are based on **EN 301 549 V3.2.1** — [TTJA digital accessibility](https://ttja.ee/eraklient/tarbija-oigused/kaubandus-teenused/digiligipaasetavuse-tagamine).
- The Commission issued standardisation request M/587 (C(2022) 6456 final, 14.9.2022) to the European standardisation organisations for harmonised standards supporting the EAA — [CEPT copy of C(2022) 6456](https://cept.org/documents/wg-nan/73985/nan-2022-11-033_mandate_587_-_sr_eaa).
- The Directive gives a presumption of conformity for products and services that meet harmonised standards published in the OJ (Art. 15). Service providers must prepare information per Annex V explaining how the service meets the accessibility requirements, make it publicly available in written and oral form accessible to persons with disabilities, and keep it as long as the service is in operation (Art. 13) — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj).
- Lithuania: mandatory accessibility requirements are set out in Annex 1 of XIV-1633. Annex 1 was amended by XV-760 from 1 April 2026 — [SADM guidelines](https://socmin.lrv.lt/public/canonical/1749467923/4905/SADM%20Prieinamumo%20istatymo%20gaires.pdf); [e-tar XV-760](https://www.e-tar.lt/portal/lt/legalAct/25be17201ed811f1a552c76556910e9c).
- Latvia: detailed requirements are in Cabinet Regulation No. 128 (27.02.2024) — [likumi.lv/ta/id/350121](https://m.likumi.lv/ta/id/350121-precu-un-pakalpojumu-pieklustamibas-prasibas). PTAC offers a self-assessment checklist for e-shops at neredzamaskerslujosla.lv — [PTAC campaign](https://www.ptac.gov.lv/lv/ptac-kampana-neredzama-skerslu-josla).
- TTJA has an accessibility-statement drafting guide. It was written for the public-sector web accessibility regime but is a useful template — [TTJA guide](https://ttja.ee/media/2486/download).

### Inferences
- That EN 301 549 v3.2.1 clause 9 maps to WCAG 2.1 Level A + AA is well known but not re-verified in this session. As of my last information, EAA-specific harmonised standards (a revised EN 301 549 under M/587) had not been cited in the OJ. Check the current OJ status before quoting "presumption of conformity".
- A practical audit deliverable is: a WCAG 2.1 AA / EN 301 549 audit plus an Annex V service-information page or accessibility statement. This matches what authorities can check most easily (inference).

### Gaps
- Whether the LT, LV or EE law or regulation explicitly names EN 301 549 or WCAG: not verified from the texts.
- National rules on the form and location of the accessibility information for services: not verified.

## 6. Enforcement / market surveillance authorities and complaint procedures

### Takeaway
- **Lithuania:** VVTAT is the lead authority (e-commerce, consumer banking, e-books). RRT covers electronic communications, the Radio and Television Commission (RTK) covers audiovisual media access, and the Transport Safety Administration covers transport.
- **Latvia:** PTAC is the market-surveillance and lead services authority. SPRK covers electronic communications and NEPLP covers audiovisual media access.
- **Estonia:** TTJA alone supervises the whole Act and handles misdemeanour proceedings.

Consumers and organisations can complain to these authorities.

### Cited Findings
- **Lithuania:**
  - VVTAT is the main supervisory authority. Three other bodies share market surveillance: the Lithuanian (Road) Transport Safety Administration, the Communications Regulatory Authority (RRT) and the Radio and Television Commission (RTK) — [VVTAT general info](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/prieinamumo-reikalavimai/bendra-informacija/); [ANTA](https://anta.lrv.lt/lt/aplinkos-prieinamumas/gaminiu-ir-paslaugu-prieinamumas/bendroji-informacija--/). ## 7. Fines and sanctions (amounts, articles)

### Takeaway
- **Lithuania:** fines of **€500–15,000** in three bands (secondary sources cite Art. 23; band 3 is tied to harm to consumer health in PwC's description), and a warning is possible for a first breach that is voluntarily corrected.
- **Estonia:** a fine of up to **€20,000** for a legal person, plus penalty payments (sunniraha) of up to **€10,000** for ignoring a precept.
- **Latvia:** only one secondary source gives a figure (**up to €10,000** for legal persons). The administrative-liability route runs through the "On Conformity Assessment" law. None of these figures were verified against the statute in this session.

### Cited Findings
- **Lithuania (PwC LT):**
  - €500–2,500 for supplying a non-compliant product or service;
  - €1,000–6,500 for disregarding a prohibition or restriction on supply;
  - €2,500–15,000 where the non-compliance caused damage to consumer health.
  - Source: [PwC Lithuania](https://www.pwc.com/lt/lt/apie-mus/naujienos/prieinamumo-reikalavimu-igyvendinimas-versle.html). An overall €500–15,000 range "depending on mitigating or aggravating circumstances" is given by [teise.pro](https://www.teise.pro/index.php/2025/05/13/nauji-reikalavimai-elektroninems-parduotuvems-kada-gresia-baudos/) and [desamedia.lt](https://www.desamedia.lt/en/insights/wcag-and-european-accessibility-act).
- **Lithuania (WCAG Auditor tracker):** cites the same three bands as **Article 23** of XIV-1633, with a warning possible for a first breach that is voluntarily corrected — [wcagauditor.com/enforcement](https://wcagauditor.com/enforcement/).
  - *Conflict:* Level Access describes the bands as €500–2,500 "for individuals" and €2,500–15,000 "for businesses" — [Level Access LT fact sheet (Apr 2025)](https://www.levelaccess.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Lithuania-Digital-Accessibility-Laws.pdf). PwC's breach-type description is more credible (Lithuanian Big-4 source).
- **Lithuania:** economic operators who breach the requirements are warned and may be fined under the procedure set by the law — [VVTAT](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/prieinamumo-reikalavimai/bendra-informacija/).
- **Estonia:** up to €20,000 fine for a legal person for non-compliance with accessibility requirements, and a penalty-payment (sunniraha) ceiling of €10,000 for failing to comply with a precept — [Ligipääsuke](http://ligipaasuke.ee/blogi/mida-uut-toob-teenuste-ja-toodete-ligipaasetavuse-seadus-ettevotetele/) / [UX Estonia](https://uxestonia.ee/blogi/ligipaasetavuse-seadus-mida-e-kaubanduse-finants-televisiooni-jt-teenuste-ja-toodete-pakkuja-teadma-peaks) (secondary). TTJA is the out-of-court misdemeanour processor — [Riigi Teataja](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/115062022001).
- **Latvia:** penalties for legal persons can reach up to €10,000 — [Ellex LV](https://ellex.legal/lv/prasibas-precu-un-pakalpojumu-pieklustamibai-uznemejiem-jagatavojas-laicigi/). This is an older (about 2023, pre-adoption) law-firm article. The draft provided for administrative liability under Art. 19(2) of the law "On Conformity Assessment" (*Par atbilstības novērtēšanu*), with PTAC conducting proceedings — [Saeima bill text](https://titania.saeima.lv/LIVS13/SaeimaLIVS13.nsf/0/B5FEC2331BE96C3AC225888000238662?OpenDocument=); [Ministry of Welfare](https://www.lm.gov.lv/lv/pieklustamibas-direktiva).
- **Directive Art. 30:** penalties must be effective, proportionate and dissuasive, and be accompanied by effective remedial action — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj).

### Inferences
- Statutory maximums are low: €15k (LT), €20k (EE) and about €10k (LV). The more serious commercial risks are probably remedy orders, prohibition or withdrawal of the service, repeated penalty payments (EE) and reputational exposure, rather than the fine itself. Sales messaging should not exaggerate fines.
- XV-760 did not list Art. 23 among the amended articles, so the LT fine bands are probably unchanged after 1 April 2026. XV-907 amended Art. 28 (content unknown). This is an inference from the amendment's article list.

### Gaps
