# Baltic SME Web Market: Website, E-shop, Redesign and Care-Plan Prices (LT / LV / EE)

> **How these notes were made, and their limits (read first).** Research date 30 Oct 2026. Direct page fetching was **blocked** in this session: paslaugos.lt and ec.europa.eu could not be reached (the hosts were not in the session's allowed network domains). The shared web-search budget then **ran out** partway through, so no more searches were possible. So every finding below comes from **search-engine result extracts** (the search tool's summary of each linked page), **not from my own reading of the full page**. Prices, dates and VAT status should be spot-checked on the linked pages before anyone relies on them in a client-facing document. Topics never searched, or left unanswered when the budget ran out, are listed under **Gaps**: agency counts, Germany and Nordics benchmarks, Lithuanian municipal grants, and SME buying-behaviour surveys. All prices are in EUR. VAT status is given where the source stated it; "VAT n/s" means the source did not say. Lithuania's standard VAT rate is cited by Oktoja as 21%.

## 1. Lithuania – published prices (landing page, 5–10 page site, e-shop, Shopify, redesign, care plans, marketplaces)

### Takeaway
Lithuania has a very transparent and very price-competitive market. Freelancers advertise sites from about €99–€300. Agencies typically quote €650–€3,000 + VAT for a 5–10 page WordPress site and €950–€2,500 for a small WooCommerce or Shopify shop. Premium SEO agencies start at €4,500+. Published monthly WordPress care plans run from €15 to €350+/month, with a cluster at €35–€99/month.

### Inferences
- *(Judgment)* The LT market is split in three. (1) A large freelancer/micro-studio layer at €99–€600 per site and €15–€50 per month for care. (2) A mid-market agency layer at €650–€3,000 + VAT per site and €50–€150 per month. (3) A premium SEO/branding layer at €4,500+ per site and €150–€800 per month. A white-label reseller using Indian delivery can undercut the mid-market layer, but will struggle to beat the €99–€300 freelancer layer on price alone.
- *(Judgment)* Published care-plan prices cluster at €35–€99/month. That looks like the realistic band for a recurring SME offer in LT. €150+ needs e-commerce, SLA or content hours included.
- *(Judgment)* Because Shopify Payments is unavailable in LT, Shopify is somewhat less attractive for very small LT merchants than WooCommerce with local payment and parcel-locker plugins.

### Gaps
- Could not open paslaugos.lt directly, so not verified: the provider count (289 is from the page title), how current the averages are, or whether listed prices include VAT.
- Could not check other freelance platforms (Upwork with an LT filter, Fiverr, Facebook groups). No LT-specific hourly data from Upwork was found.
- Most agency pages did not state VAT status in the extracts. Only Oktoja, Kasakai, EASYSEO and Wordorado explicitly said "excl. VAT".

## 2. Latvia – published prices

Latvian agency starting prices are noticeably higher than Lithuanian ones. WordPress business sites typically start at €1,200–€2,200 + VAT, Shopify and WooCommerce shops at €1,400–€2,800, and published care plans run €30–€150/month (up to €600 for e-commerce). ## 4. DIY / website-builder and local SaaS alternatives

### Takeaway
Each Baltic country has strong local low-cost alternatives. Estonia has **Voog** (from €1.25–€39/month) and **ShopRoller** (from about €29/month). Latvia has **Mozello** (free to about €12/month, with local bank payments and parcel lockers). Lithuania has a cluster of rented e-shop platforms, including **Verskis.lt** (€31–€135/month), ParduotuvesNuoma.lt, eShoprent and Shopium. These set a low price anchor that custom builds must justify.

### Cited Findings
- **Voog (EE):** Starter €1.25/month (annual billing), Standard €11, Plus €17, Premium €39 per month. Monthly billing costs more (e.g. Standard €14, Plus €22). ## 8. EU / state grants for SME websites, e-commerce and digitalisation (2025–2026)

### Inferences — Saturation assessment *(my judgment)*
- **Lithuania – High saturation, strong price pressure.** About 289 providers on one marketplace category, sites advertised from €99–€300, care plans from €15/month, many 2026 SEO-driven price articles, and about 12–18k webshops (mostly WooCommerce). The opportunity is in the mid-market (€800–€2,500 sites, €49–€99/month care) with better quality, speed and add-ons such as accessibility compliance, not in competing on lowest price. It is the largest Baltic market and the client's home market, so it is a sensible first market despite the saturation.
- **Latvia – Medium saturation.** Agency entry prices are notably higher (about €1,200–€1,500 for a WordPress site, €1,400+ for Shopify). Fewer ultra-cheap public offers surfaced. Website adoption is the lowest in the Baltics (68.8%). But it has the smallest webshop base (about 5k in 2024) and a strong local DIY tool (Mozello). There is a price gap to exploit with a €700–€1,000 white-label offer.
- **Estonia – Medium-high saturation.** Highly transparent pricing (published medians), low entry prices (€329–€350), strong local SaaS (Voog, ShopRoller) and WooCommerce dominance (about 65%) all compress margins. It is a digitally sophisticated market where local payment integrations (Montonio, bank links) are expected.
- **Across all three:** WooCommerce-first, Shopify-second fits the installed base. Grants are not currently a reliable sales lever (section 8).

### Gaps
- The table relies on search extracts, not on directly fetched pages. The redesign rows lack data for LV and EE. The Shopify "low" figure for EE is missing. VAT status of many marketplace and freelancer prices is unknown.
- No agency-count data was found for any country, and there are no Germany or Nordics benchmarks (sections 6 and 9).
