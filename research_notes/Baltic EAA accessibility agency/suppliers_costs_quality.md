Every figure below therefore comes from **WebSearch result summaries and snippets**, not from pages I opened myself. Each figure links to the page the search engine attributed it to. ## 1. Accessibility supply prices (audits, remediation, statements/ACR, monitoring), named Indian suppliers, marketplace rates

### Takeaway
Offshore accessibility work is cheap at the freelancer level: Indian WCAG specialists on Upwork list about USD 14–25/h, and Fiverr "manual audit" gigs cost about USD 50–100. Indian firms that publish prices charge about USD 500 for 5 pages up to USD 2,400–5,900 for a 12–25-page audit with screen-reader testing. That is roughly 0.3–2x what US specialist Accessible.org charges, so the "Indian discount" for a credible firm audit is smaller than most people assume. BarrierBreak, the largest India-based specialist (300+ staff), runs a partner programme for digital agencies but publishes no prices. No Indian supplier I found publishes white-label remediation or monitoring prices.

### Inferences
- A sensible supplier range for a **manual WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA audit of a typical Baltic SME site (about 10–15 unique templates)** is: **USD 250–700** from a vetted individual Upwork freelancer (my estimate: about 15–30 hours at USD 14–25/h), **USD 2,400** (≤12 pages, thefrontkit) to **USD 1,500–5,000** (DigitalA11Y) from a firm with published prices, and a probably similar range from BarrierBreak-class firms (quote needed). Fiverr's USD 50–100 gigs cannot cover a real multi-template manual audit, so treat them as automated scans with a light manual pass.
- Indian firm audit prices overlap Western boutique prices (Accessible.org USD 1,250–2,750). Most of the margin will come from buying from **individual certified freelancers or small studios**, not established firms, which raises the importance of QA (§7).
- Price remediation by the hour or as a capped block of hours tied to an audit issue list, not per site. No credible flat per-site price exists.
- An EU **accessibility statement** is mainly a fill-in-the-template document built from the audit results. The supplier's part is small; the local-language drafting and translation (§6) is the bigger cost. An ACR/VPAT matters only if a Baltic client sells to public-sector buyers. EN 301 549 edition ACRs cost more (USD 650 vs 350 at Accessible.org).

### Gaps
- No Indian supplier published a price for **monitoring**, **accessibility statements**, **ACRs**, or **per-issue remediation**.
- I could not open BarrierBreak's partner page to see whether it lists tiers or minimums. Ask partner@barrierbreak.com directly.
- I found no Upwork/Fiverr listings that could be filtered to verify IAAP **WAS** (as opposed to CPACC) certification, or rate premiums for certified freelancers. Only one IAAP-certified profile (USD 14/h, Upwork) and one (USD 35/h, Freelancer.com) appeared.
- JAWS testing by an Indian vendor was not confirmed in the results. thefrontkit cites NVDA and VoiceOver; one Fiverr gig claims NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver and TalkBack.

## 2. Web development supply prices (India white-label WordPress/WooCommerce and Shopify; care plans; Upwork rates)

### Takeaway
Indian white-label WordPress capacity costs about USD 15–35/h (Upwork median for WordPress developers is USD 15–28/h; Indian web developers USD 16–35/h; Dynamic Dreamz white-label USD 20/h). Published package prices are scarce: one white-label provider (Seahawk) starts sites at USD 999. White-label Shopify builds from an India-based provider (Codingclave) cost USD 600–3,500 per store plus USD 300/month store care. White-label WordPress care plans cost USD 39–99 per site per month wholesale.

### Inferences
- **5–10-page WordPress business site (theme-based, client supplies content):** about USD 600–1,500 supplier cost (my estimate: 30–60 hours at USD 20–25/h; Seahawk's USD 999 starter sits mid-range).
- **Small e-shop (WooCommerce or Shopify, ≤50 products, theme-based):** about USD 600–2,500 (Codingclave's ranges; Indian direct prices ₹45–85k ≈ EUR 430–810 suggest a white-label markup of about 1.5x).
- **Care plan:** USD 39–99/site/month wholesale for WordPress, falling with volume; USD 300/month for Shopify "store care" from Codingclave. Care plans are where volume discounts (10/25/50-site tiers) matter.
- Building accessibility into a new site costs much less than fixing it afterwards. The brief to the web supplier should name WCAG 2.2 AA explicitly and require an accessible theme, so the client doesn't need a separate remediation project. This is general good practice, not sourced here.

- No India-specific Upwork rate page for WordPress or Shopify came up (only global WordPress and Indian "web developer" pages).
- I could not verify whether Seahawk's USD 49 and USD 999 are white-label (agency) prices or retail prices.

## 3. Marketplace and payment costs (Upwork, Fiverr, Wise, Payoneer)

### Takeaway
Add about **5% + USD 0.99–14.99 per contract** on Upwork Basic (10% on Business Plus), or **5.5% plus a small-order fee** on Fiverr. A direct bank transfer EUR→INR via Wise costs about **0.6–0.7%**. Indian suppliers usually absorb their own receiving costs (Payoneer about 3–4%, Wise about 1.8% plus GST per a competitor's claim) and price them in.

### Cited Findings
- **Upwork Contract Initiation Fee**, from Upwork's support centre: a **one-time fee of USD 0.99–14.99 per new Marketplace or Project Catalog contract**. It applies even when rehiring the same freelancer, but **not to new milestones on an existing fixed-price contract**. The amount varies with contract type, account history and hiring activity. On Business Plus, contracts of **USD 100 or less** carry the fee. — [Upwork Help: Contract Initiation Fee](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/26106318334611-What-is-the-Contract-Initiation-Fee-on-Upwork)
- **Upwork client marketplace fee**, from third-party 2026 guides: **5% on the Basic plan** (3% for eligible US clients paying by ACH) and **10% on Business Plus** (8% with ACH). — [GoLance 2026](https://golance.com/blogs/upwork-fees-explained-2026); [HireInSouth 2026](https://www.hireinsouth.com/post/how-much-does-upwork-cost). This **conflicts** with another HireInSouth page describing a "5% Contract Initiation Fee on the first $5,000" — [HireInSouth review](https://www.hireinsouth.com/post/upwork-review) — and with older sources citing up to 7.99%. Upwork's official pricing page ([upwork.com/pricing/client](https://www.upwork.com/pricing/client)) could not be opened.
- Upwork has a support article titled "Offer: Get a reduced Marketplace fee as an active client on our Upwork Basic plan", which suggests volume discounts exist. I could not read its terms. — [Upwork Help](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/49110927628307-Offer-Get-a-reduced-Marketplace-fee-as-an-active-client-on-our-Upwork-Basic-plan)
- **Fiverr buyer fee**: **5.5% service fee** on the order, consistent across sources. — [HireInSouth Fiverr pricing](https://hireinsouth.com/post/fiverr-pricing). The small-order fee is disputed: a ConductAtlas excerpt of Fiverr's terms says **5.5% + USD 0.50 (minimum USD 2.50) for orders up to USD 200**, then 5.5% above that — [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/fiverr/fiverr-terms-of-service/buyer-service-fee-structure/). Another guide says a flat USD 3.50 under USD 200 — [Gerald](https://joingerald.com/learn/work--income/how-much-does-fiverr-take).
- **Wise (sender side, from Ireland)**: Wise's own example puts the total cost of sending **EUR 30,000 to INR at EUR 174.62 from a Wise balance, or EUR 217.38 by bank transfer**, at the mid-market rate with fees shown upfront. That works out to **≈0.58% / ≈0.72%** (my calculation). Business-account pricing was not found. — [Wise send money (IE)](https://www.wise.com/ie/send-money)
- EUR→INR rates on Wise pages in the result set ranged from **≈103.9 to ≈110.4 INR per EUR** (different dates). — [Wise converter](https://wise.com/sk/currency-converter/eur-to-inr-rate?amount=250)
- **Receiving side, India (supplier's cost; competitor marketing)**: Skydo says Indian businesses pay **about 1.8% plus 18% GST on that fee plus USD 2 per FIRA** (the foreign-inward remittance certificate Indian banks issue) when receiving via Wise. — [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/compare/wise-fee-calculator-eur-to-inr)
- **Payoneer (India receiving)**: no receiving fee for marketplace payouts, but a **1–4% withdrawal/conversion cost to INR**; about 1% for ACH bank debits from clients; **USD 29.95/year inactivity fee**. All-in about 3–4% per typical USD→INR payout (competitor and blog sources). — [Skydo on Payoneer](https://www.skydo.com/compare/payoneer-pricing); [Wise India blog](https://wise.com/in/blog/payoneer-vs-paypal)

### Inferences
- **Landed cost formula** (my framework): supplier price × (1 + marketplace fee) + per-contract fee + transfer/FX cost (direct contracts) + translation (§6) + tool allocation (§4) + QA allowance (§7).
- Example (my estimate): a USD 2,400 audit bought directly from an Indian firm and paid via Wise costs about USD 2,415. The same USD 2,400 contract on Upwork Basic costs about USD 2,520 + up to USD 15 ≈ USD 2,535. Upwork's 5% buys escrow, dispute handling and a work history. For first jobs with unknown suppliers that is probably worth it; for proven suppliers, moving to direct contracts saves about 5%. Check the Upwork Terms of Service before taking a freelancer off-platform: they include a conversion/"opt-out" fee for doing so. I did not verify that clause this session; it is from memory.
- Paying in USD rather than EUR is the norm for Indian suppliers. The USD/EUR exchange risk falls on the Estonian reseller if client prices are in EUR, so build a 2–3% FX buffer (my estimate).

### Gaps
- Upwork's official client pricing page and its fixed-price **escrow / milestone / dispute** mechanics could not be verified (search budget exhausted, fetch blocked).
- A **0% marketplace-fee promotion for certain categories (9 Apr–28 Aug 2026)** appeared in a search summary, but I could not tie it to a specific page. It has expired in any case.
- No Wise **Business** EUR→INR fee schedule was found. The figure above is the consumer example.

## 4. Tools: automated scanning/monitoring, free vs paid, agency / white-label plans, "free scan" lead magnets

### Takeaway
For a small reseller, the cheap and credible monitoring options are **Pope Tech** (Team USD 25/month; 1–1,000 pages USD 150/month annual), **Rocket Validator** (axe-core; Micro EUR 19, Lite EUR 29, Agency EUR 119/month), **WAVE API** pay-per-page (USD 0.025–0.04 per page) for a home-made "free scan" lead magnet, and **Equalize Digital Accessibility Checker** for WordPress (Agency USD 2,250/year for 25 sites). Siteimprove (about USD 12,000–40,000/year) and Monsido/Acquia (from about USD 2,000 per feature per year) are enterprise-priced. Several cheap SaaS tools sell white-label PDFs and "compliance certificates", which is a quality red flag.

## 5. How much do automated tools detect, and therefore how much manual work does a real audit need?

### Takeaway
Automated tools find roughly **13–40% of known barriers** in the UK government's controlled test, up to 50% if you count warnings that need a human to judge. Deque's vendor study claims they find **57% of issues by volume**. Either way, at least about 40–60% of problems need human testing: keyboard, screen reader, content meaning, alt-text quality, forms and errors. The WebAIM Million 2026 shows that **95.9% of top home pages fail automatically detectable checks**, averaging 56.1 errors, so almost every Baltic SME prospect will "fail" a free scan.

### Cited Findings
- **Deque (vendor, March 2021, older than 2025):** on average **57% of accessibility issues** were fully covered by automated testing (axe). The data came from **2,000+ first-time audits, 13,000+ pages and about 300,000 issues**. Deque contrasts this with the "widely accepted belief" of 20–30% coverage. The higher figure comes from measuring by **issue volume rather than share of WCAG success criteria**. axe-core deliberately avoids false positives. No independent replication found. — [Deque blog](https://www.deque.com/blog/automated-testing-study-identifies-57-percent-of-digital-accessibility-issues); [DevOps.com](https://devops.com/deque-study-shows-its-automated-testing-identifies-57-percent-of-digital-accessibility-issues-surpassing-accepted-industry-benchmarks/)
- **UK Government Digital Service tool audit (undated; archived copy from May 2026):** **142 barriers** deliberately placed on a page and run through **13 tools**. The best tool found **40%** and the worst **13%**. Counting "potential barriers needing human review", the best reached **50%**. — [GDS accessibility tool audit (UK Gov Web Archive copy)](https://tnaqa.mirrorweb.com/ukgwa/20260506110838mp_/https://alphagov.github.io/accessibility-tool-audit/); original at [alphagov.github.io/accessibility-tool-audit](https://alphagov.github.io/accessibility-tool-audit/)
- The 2017 predecessor tested 10 tools against 143 failures, and **42 failures were missed by every tool**. — [GOV.UK accessibility blog, 2017](https://accessibility.blog.gov.uk/2017/02/24/what-we-found-when-we-tested-tools-on-the-worlds-least-accessible-webpage/)
- The UK DWP Accessibility Manual cites the GDS audit: the best tools found **about 30–40% of the 142 known issues**, so manual testing is still required. — [DWP Accessibility Manual](https://accessibility-manual.dwp.gov.uk/tools-and-resources/automated-accessibility-testing)
- **WebAIM Million 2026 (tested Feb 2026; figures via secondary summaries; primary page not opened):** **95.9%** of the top 1,000,000 home pages had detectable WCAG 2 failures, up from **94.8% in 2025**, ending six years of slow improvement. **56.1 errors per page on average (+10.1%)**. Most common: **low-contrast text 83.9%, missing alt text 53.1%, missing form labels 51.0%**; the six most common error types make up **96%** of detected errors. Average page elements **1,437 (+22.5%)**. Home pages using ARIA averaged **59.1 errors vs 42.0** without (single secondary source). — [WebAIM Million (primary)](https://webaim.org/projects/million/); [Priority Pixels](https://prioritypixels.co.uk/insights/web-accessibility-is-getting-worse-according-to-the-2026-webaim-million-report/); [Nexer Digital](https://www.nexerdigital.com/news-and-thoughts/the-webaim-million-report-2026-what-it-tells-us); [Aaron Gustafson](https://www.aaron-gustafson.com/notebook/links/the-webaim-million-the-2026-report-on-the-accessibility-of-the-top-1000000-home-pages/)
- **Screen readers to test with: WebAIM Screen Reader User Survey #10** (Dec 2023–Jan 2024, 1,539 respondents; older than 2025). Any-use shares: **NVDA 65.6%, JAWS 60.5%, VoiceOver 43.9%**. Top pairings: **JAWS+Chrome 24.7%, NVDA+Chrome 21.3%, JAWS+Edge 11.4%, NVDA+Firefox 10.0%, VoiceOver+Safari 7.0%**. Primary-desktop shares reported by third parties (JAWS 40.5%, NVDA 37.7%) could not be verified. — [WebAIM SR Survey #10](https://webaim.org/projects/screenreadersurvey10/); [Accessibility Developer Guide](https://www.accessibility-developer-guide.com/knowledge/screen-readers/relevant-combinations/)

### Inferences
- A supplier audit that lists only axe/WAVE/Lighthouse findings covers at most about 40–57% of issues. Contracts should require proof of manual testing: a keyboard-only walkthrough, **NVDA + Chrome** (free, matching the most common pairing) and **VoiceOver + Safari on iOS** for mobile, with findings mapped to WCAG success criteria.
- Rough manual-effort rule of thumb (my estimate, unsourced): a manual audit of one unique template plus key flows (cart/checkout, contact form) takes about 1–3 hours of specialist time. That fits thefrontkit's USD 2,400 for 12 pages plus a flow plus a retest.
- Because about 96% of automatically detected errors fall into six easy categories (contrast, alt text, labels, empty links/buttons, language), much **first-pass remediation is cheap work** for a WordPress or Shopify developer. Fixing keyboard traps, custom widgets and checkout flows needs more skill.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 independent replication of the Deque 57% figure was found. I could not open the WebAIM 2026 primary page to confirm the secondary figures.

## 7. Vetting and management playbook for a non-technical reseller (finding, testing, contracting, QA, security)

### Takeaway
A non-developer can manage quality with five controls: **(1) a paid test audit on a page with known answers** (the UK GDS test page has 142 documented barriers); **(2) red-flag screening** (overlays, automated-only reports, "100% compliant" certificates); **(3) milestone or escrow payments tied to deliverables and a free re-test**; **(4) independent checks**: an automated re-scan with your own tool, W3C "Easy Checks" and basic keyboard and screen-reader spot checks, plus an occasional second-opinion audit; **(5) access hygiene**: staging sites, named accounts, revocable credentials.

### Cited Findings
- **Certification signals:** IAAP's **CPACC** (core competencies) and **WAS** (web accessibility specialist) are the standard credentials. Exam fees reported by third parties conflict: **USD 345 member / 495 non-member** (TestParty); **USD 430 / 540** (Practice Test Geeks); **USD 410 / 510** (Open Exam Prep). Fees are reportedly **about halved for candidates from emerging economies** (Substack). — [TestParty](https://testparty.ai/blog/accessibility-certifications); [Practice Test Geeks](https://practicetestgeeks.com/cpacc/amp); [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/books/exams/iaap-cpacc); [Double A11y Substack](https://doublea11y.substack.com/p/cracking-the-cpacc-exam); background — [BOIA: what is CPACC](https://www.boia.org/blog/what-is-a-cpacc-accessibility-certification)
- Some Indian firms advertise **DEPwD empanelment** (Indian government approved list) as a credential. — [Levitation list](https://levitation.in/top-accessibility-audit-companies-in-india)
- **Overlay red flag, regulatory evidence:** the US FTC finalised an order in **April 2025** requiring **accessiBe to pay USD 1 million**. The order bars it from claiming its automated products can make any website WCAG-compliant or keep it compliant over time without evidence. It was proposed 3 Jan 2025. — [FTC press release](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-approves-final-order-requiring-accessibe-pay-1-million); [Adrian Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2025/01/ftc-catches-up-to-accessibe.html)
- The **European Disability Forum** has published a "Joint statement on accessibility overlays" (content not retrieved this session). — [EDF](https://www.edf-feph.org/publications/joint-statement-on-accessibility-overlays/). The **Overlay Fact Sheet**, a community statement maintained by Karl Groves, has 700 to 1,000+ signatories depending on the source date. — [overview via Freelock](https://www.freelock.com/advent/2025/13-accessibility-overlay-trap-why-one-line-code-wont-save-you)
- A remediation-vendor competitor claims **800+ businesses using accessiBe were still sued** (US). This is a competitor's claim; treat with caution. — [TestParty](https://testparty.ai/blog/why-800-businesses-with-accessibe-were-still-sued)
- **"Certificate" red flag:** some cheap scanning SaaS products market "time-stamped compliance certificates" and white-label PDFs to agencies. — [Postmake on AccessibilityChecker.org](https://postmake.io/tools/accessibilitychecker-org)
- **A reference test page with known answers:** the GDS tool-audit page contains **142 deliberately introduced barriers** with documented results. — [GDS accessibility tool audit](https://alphagov.github.io/accessibility-tool-audit/)
- **Layperson checks:** W3C WAI's **Easy Checks – A First Review of Web Accessibility** gives "simple steps for anyone who can use the Web; no accessibility knowledge or skill is required". It covers only a few issues and is "not definitive". Most checks work in any browser. Updated Dec 2016, so older, but still current W3C guidance. — [W3C Easy Checks](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/preliminary/)
- **Re-test pricing norm:** DigitalA11Y prices retests at about **half the original audit fee**; thefrontkit's 12-page package **includes one retest**. — [DigitalA11Y](https://www.digitala11y.com/pricing/); [thefrontkit](https://thefrontkit.com/blogs/accessibility-audit-cost-2026)
- **White-label delivery norms:** EU-based WCAG Accessibility Partner advertises "NDA-first white-label delivery"; WCAGconsultant says the agency "retains full client and project ownership". On Shopify, Codingclave's model keeps the **Shopify Partner account and client relationship with the agency**, with supplier developers working as users inside it. — [WCAG Partner EU](https://wcagpartner.eu/); [WCAGconsultant](https://www.wcagconsultant.com/); [Codingclave](https://codingclave.com/usa/services/white-label-shopify-development)
- **Upwork contract mechanics relevant to milestones:** adding milestones to an existing fixed-price contract does not trigger a new Contract Initiation Fee. Structure one contract with several milestones rather than many small contracts. — [Upwork Help](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/26106318334611-What-is-the-Contract-Initiation-Fee-on-Upwork)

### Inferences (the playbook; my recommendations, built on the findings above)
**A. Finding candidates (shortlist 5–8 per category)**
- Accessibility: BarrierBreak (partner@barrierbreak.com), thefrontkit, DigitalA11Y, plus 3–5 Upwork freelancers who state **CPACC/WAS with a verifiable IAAP credential** and show sample reports. Keep one EU white-label firm (e.g., WCAG Accessibility Partner) as a **second-opinion auditor**.
- Web: 2–3 white-label firms with Clutch reviews (Codingclave, Dynamic Dreamz, Seahawk) plus 2–3 Upwork agencies with long WordPress/Shopify histories.
- Ask every candidate for: a redacted sample audit report, the screen reader and browser combinations they test with, how they map findings to WCAG success criteria, their re-test policy, and who will actually do the work (names, CVs, certificates).

**B. Paid test project (pay USD 100–300 each; my estimate)**
- Give each accessibility candidate the **same pages**: the **GDS 142-barrier test page** (answers known) plus 3–5 pages of a real Baltic e-shop. Score by **share of known barriers found**, clarity of fix instructions, false positives, and whether keyboard and screen-reader findings appear at all. Tools alone find about 13–40% on the GDS page, so a candidate who finds well above 40% is clearly testing manually.
- For web candidates: a small paid task on a **staging copy**, e.g. build one page from a Figma/PDF to WCAG 2.2 AA. Then check it with your own scanner plus a keyboard test.

**C. Red flags (any one disqualifies)**
- Proposes an overlay or widget as "the fix", or promises "100% compliant" or a "compliance certificate".
- The report contains only automated rule IDs (axe/WAVE) with no keyboard, screen-reader or content findings.
- Won't name the testers, can't show certificates, or the audit price is far below about USD 15–25 per hour of plausible effort.
- Asks for live-site admin passwords for an audit (audits need no admin access), or refuses an NDA or non-solicitation clause.

**D. Contract terms checklist**
- Scope: page or template list, user flows, WCAG version and level (2.2 AA; EN 301 549 for public-sector-type clients), testing tools and screen readers, deliverable format (issue list with SC reference, severity, location, screenshot, fix advice), **one free re-test within N days**.
- White-label: no supplier branding, supplier never contacts the end client, **non-solicitation of clients for 12–24 months**, confidentiality/NDA, IP assignment of code and reports to the reseller on payment, GDPR data-processing terms if the supplier sees personal data (EU law requires this when a processor handles personal data; not sourced here).
- Payment: milestones (e.g., 30/40/30) released on acceptance criteria; on Upwork, one fixed-price contract with multiple milestones.
- Warranty: fixes regressed within 30–60 days are re-done free.

**E. Independent QA the salesperson can do personally (about 30–60 minutes per site)**
- Re-scan with your own tool (Pope Tech, Rocket Validator or WAVE). Automated errors should fall close to zero on fixed templates.
- W3C Easy Checks: page titles, image alt text, headings, contrast, resize/zoom, keyboard access and visible focus, forms and labels.
- **Keyboard-only walkthrough** of menu, search, product, cart, checkout and contact form: Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, Space, Esc. Focus must always be visible and never trapped.
- **NVDA (free) + Chrome** and **VoiceOver on an iPhone**: can you hear the form labels and error messages in the local language, and is the page language set correctly for LT/LV/ET?
- About once per 5–10 audits, or for flagship clients, buy a **second-opinion audit** of 3–5 pages from a different supplier and compare.

**F. Time zone and communication**
- India (IST, UTC+5:30, no daylight saving) is **3.5 hours ahead of Estonia in winter and 2.5 hours ahead in summer**. Working days overlap by about 5–6 hours. This is my calculation from standard time-zone offsets.
- Use written briefs, Loom or screen-recorded walkthroughs, and a shared issue tracker. Hold one weekly call. Require a named backup person at agencies to reduce turnover risk.

**G. Security and access**
- Never share the client's own admin login. Create **named, least-privilege accounts** per supplier (WordPress role; Shopify staff or collaborator account). Work on a **staging copy**. Share credentials through a password manager, enforce 2FA, and **revoke access at project end**. Keep the domain, hosting, Shopify ownership and backups in the reseller's or client's name.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the **IAAP certification directory** (search or verify a holder) or IAAP's official 2026 fees. The third-party fee figures conflict.
- Upwork's **escrow, auto-release and dispute** rules, and any **off-platform conversion fee** in the Upwork Terms, were not verified this session.
- No source-backed template for white-label **non-solicitation** clauses or typical durations was found.

## 8. Typical failure modes when reselling offshore web / accessibility work

### Takeaway
The few quantitative sources point to **communication problems (about 21%), missed deadlines (about 14%) and failure to follow instructions (about 12%)** as the most common outsourcing complaints. For accessibility specifically, the documented failure mode is **overlays or automated-only "fixes" sold as compliance** (FTC accessiBe order). I found no rigorous study of reseller-specific failures such as freelancer disappearance or staff turnover.

### Cited Findings
- An outsourcing study summarised by Oberlo (study date not shown): **21%** of brands say outsourced teams are difficult to communicate with, **14%** cite failure to meet deadlines, **12%** failure to follow instructions. — [Oberlo: challenges of outsourcing](https://www.oberlo.com/statistics/challenges-of-outsourcing)
- Fund-administration outsourcing research (Ocorian) names **poor communication and late information requests** as the biggest outsourcing frustrations. This is a different sector; details not retrieved. — [Ocorian press release](https://www.ocorian.com/press-releases/poor-communication-late-information-requests-biggest-outsourcing-frustrations-says-new-research)
- Accessibility-specific: the FTC found accessiBe's claims that its widget makes sites WCAG-compliant unsupported, and ordered a USD 1M payment (April 2025). — [FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-approves-final-order-requiring-accessibe-pay-1-million)
- Automated tools miss most barriers (best 40% / worst 13% in the GDS test). An automated-only "audit" will miss most real problems. — [GDS tool audit](https://alphagov.github.io/accessibility-tool-audit/)

### Inferences
- Likely failure modes and mitigations (my synthesis):
  1. **Automated-only audit sold as manual.** Mitigate with the GDS test page, required screen-reader evidence (short recordings or notes per flow), and spot checks.
  2. **"Fixes" that are overlays or CSS hacks.** Ban overlays contractually and re-scan plus keyboard-test after fixes.
  3. **Regression after later updates** (theme or plugin updates in care plans). Monitor monthly and include accessibility regression checks in the care-plan scope.
  4. **Freelancer disappears mid-project.** Use milestone escrow, keep code in the reseller's Git or staging, and keep a backup supplier.
  5. **Turnover at agencies** (a new developer doesn't know the client). Require named staff plus a backup, and keep written runbooks per client.
  6. **Local-language gaps.** English alt text or error messages on a Lithuanian site. Translation is a separate line item with native review (§6).
  7. **Scope creep and misunderstood briefs.** Use written briefs with screenshots and acceptance criteria.
  8. **Security incidents from shared admin logins.** Use named accounts, staging and revocation (§7G).

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 survey specifically on **white-label web agency or reseller failures**, **freelancer abandonment rates on Upwork**, or **offshore accessibility audit quality** was found. Deloitte's most recent outsourcing survey surfaced only as a 2022 talent-acquisition figure, outside the date range and off-topic.

Add 5% plus up to USD 15 per contract on Upwork, or about 0.6–0.7% via Wise.

### Inferences
- **Worked landed-cost example, "EAA starter package" for one 12-template WooCommerce shop** (all **my estimates** built from the table): audit USD 2,400 (firm) or about USD 500 (vetted freelancer) + remediation about USD 500 + translation of statement, alt text and errors into Lithuanian (about 3,000 words × EUR 0.05 ≈ EUR 150) + Upwork fees (5% ≈ USD 50–150) + QA allowance (say 10% for a periodic second opinion) ≈ **EUR 1,300–3,500 total supplier cost**. Monitoring is then about EUR 10–30/month per site in tools plus 0.5–1 hour of review.
- Convert INR at about 105–110 INR/EUR (the range seen on Wise pages, Oct 2026 search results) and confirm the live rate before quoting.
- The largest cost uncertainty is **remediation hours**. It depends on the theme and plugins, and only an audit reveals it. Sell fixes as a fixed "block of hours" or a quote after the audit, never as a blind fixed price.
