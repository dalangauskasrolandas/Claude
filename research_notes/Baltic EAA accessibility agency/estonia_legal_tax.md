## 1. Ways to start in Estonia (OÜ, FIE, entrepreneur account, the first project before the company exists) and the recommended path

### Takeaway
An OÜ costs a €265 state fee through the e-Business Register (company portal) and can be registered in about a day. Since the 2023 reform there is no minimum share capital: the minimum is €0.01 per share. The entrepreneur account (ettevõtluskonto) taxes **gross receipts** at 20% with no deduction for costs, up to €40,000 a year. That makes it a poor vehicle for reselling Indian-sourced services. **Judgment:** found the OÜ before the first contract or invoice, not after the first payment.

### Cited Findings
- **OÜ state fee, fast-track e-Business Register: €265.** Registration is possible within about a day after signing digitally and paying by bank link on the company portal (ettevõtjaportaal). — [enty.io, "OÜ asutamine 2026"](https://enty.io/blog/ou-asutamine-2026-samm-sammuline-protsess); [ariportaal.ee, "Firma loomise sammud"](https://ariportaal.ee/firma-loomise-sammud/)
- **Notary route:** state fee €200, plus a notary fee usually around €100–200 plus VAT (secondary source). — [ariportaal.ee](https://ariportaal.ee/firma-loomise-sammud/). The Chamber of Notaries says the notary fee and the state fee are set separately and should be confirmed with the notary office. — [notar.ee, "Ühingutega seotud toimingud"](https://www.notar.ee/et/teabekeskus/ariuhing)
- Older pages quoting €190 or €145 are outdated. I found **no official 2026 fee schedule** (the search tool could not surface one from riigiteataja.ee or rik.ee). — [search synthesis of the sources above; ariportaal.ee](https://ariportaal.ee/ettevotte-asutamise-kulud/)
- **Share capital after the 2023 reform:** the minimum share capital requirement was abolished, but each share's nominal value must be at least one cent. — [ariportaal.ee, "Ettevõtte asutamise kulud"](https://ariportaal.ee/ettevotte-asutamise-kulud/); [jurist.stabimer.ee](https://jurist.stabimer.ee/ettevotte_registreerimine_eestis/osauhingu/)
- **Entrepreneur account rate:** since 1 January 2025 the 20% rate applies to all receipts up to €40,000 a year. The former 40% rate on the €25,000–40,000 band was abolished. — [fin.ee, "Ettevõtluskonto kõrgem maksumäär kaob aastast 2025"](https://www.fin.ee/uudised/ettevotluskonto-korgem-maksumaar-kaob-aastast-2025); [EMTA news, "Using an entrepreneur account became more favourable"](https://www.emta.ee/en/news/using-entrepreneur-account-became-more-favourable)
- **Entrepreneur account in 2026:** the planned rise was cancelled and the rate stays at 20%. It is 22%, 24% or 26% if the person has joined the II pillar pension, because the rate includes income tax, social tax and the funded-pension contribution. — [EMTA, "Maksumuudatused 2026. aastal"](https://www.emta.ee/uudised/maksumuudatused-2026); [fin.ee, Taxes](https://fin.ee/en/taxes)
- **The entrepreneur account tax applies to the whole amount received.** There is no cost deduction. — [EMTA, "Maksumuudatused 2026" (via search summary)](https://www.emta.ee/uudised/maksumuudatused-2026); [EMTA, "ETTEVÕTLUS, ETTEVÕTLUSKONTO" PDF](https://www.emta.ee/media/1981/download)
- **€40,000 cap:** if receipts exceed €40,000 in a calendar year, the person must register as an entrepreneur (FIE or company) in the Commercial Register and as a VAT payer. — [EMTA (via search summary)](https://www.emta.ee/en/news/using-entrepreneur-account-became-more-favourable); [fin.ee, Taxes](https://fin.ee/en/taxes)
- **Legal-person payers:** when a legal person pays a service fee to someone's entrepreneur account, the legal person must also pay income tax at 22/78 on that fee (TSD annex 6, code 6080). This does not apply to payments for goods. — [EMTA FIE handbook "Füüsilisest isikust ettevõtja ABC"](https://www.emta.ee/node/125/handbook/7/pdf); [EMTA entrepreneur-account PDF](https://www.emta.ee/media/1981/download)
- **Health insurance:** coverage through the entrepreneur account reportedly requires receipts of €2,436.50 a month in 2026. This came from a search summary attributed to EMTA and was not checked against the page. It is largely irrelevant here, because the person is already insured through employment. — [EMTA, "Maksumuudatused 2026"](https://www.emta.ee/uudised/maksumuudatused-2026)

### Inferences
- **Entrepreneur account economics for resale (my estimate, illustrative):** on a €5,000 client fee with €2,500 paid to the Indian supplier, the entrepreneur account tax is 20% × €5,000 = €1,000, leaving a €1,500 margin. That is before any Estonian reverse-charge VAT on the Indian purchase (24% × €2,500 = €600, if registration as a limited taxable person applies; see section 3), which would cut the margin to €900. Through a VAT-registered OÜ, the same €2,500 margin stays untaxed in the company. If it is paid out as a dividend, CIT is 22% of the gross amount (€550), leaving €1,950 net, less accounting costs.
- The 22/78 surcharge on legal-person payers is an Estonian TSD obligation. Whether it reaches a **foreign** (Lithuanian) company with no Estonian presence is unclear. I found no source either way. Even if it does not, Lithuanian procurement teams may prefer, or require, a counterparty with a company code and VAT number. (Judgment.)
- **Recommended lowest-risk path (JUDGMENT, not advice):**
  1. When the first Lithuanian client agrees in principle, issue a non-binding proposal or letter of intent from you personally.
  2. Found the OÜ through the company portal (€265, about one business day).
  3. Have the OÜ sign the client contract, sign the Indian supplier contract, and issue the invoice.
  4. Apply for voluntary VAT registration at once (reasons in section 3).
  5. Do not pay the Indian supplier, or invoice the client, in your personal name.

  Waiting for the first payment before founding the company buys little: the cost is €265 plus later annual reporting. It also creates awkward pre-incorporation liability and tax questions.
- Pre-incorporation contracting is possible in principle, as an "OÜ in formation" with an assignment or novation clause. But a lawyer must confirm the liability of the person acting (see Gaps). Founding first is simpler.

### Gaps

Unverified background: the people who acted are jointly liable until the registered company approves the transaction. A lawyer should confirm.
- **FIE (sole proprietor) taxes:** the EMTA FIE page could not be fetched. Unverified background: income tax is 22% on business profit, and social tax is 33% on profit, with a minimum monthly social-tax base tied to the minimum wage and specific exemptions. The account is opened only with banks that have an agreement with EMTA (LHV has historically been the main one; unverified).
- **Annual report and accounting obligations for an OÜ** (filing deadline, micro-entity format, whether a dormant or nil report is needed) were not researched. ## 2. Estonian tax rates 2025–2026 (and anything scheduled for 2027)

### Takeaway
For 2026, personal income tax stays at **22%**. The basic exemption is a flat **€700 a month** for everyone, so the income-dependent "tax hump" is gone. Corporate income tax on distributions stays at **22/78**. The planned 2% "security tax" was dropped, and the planned rise of income tax to 24% from 2026 was cancelled. I found **no adopted 2027 changes**.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 income tax rate: 22%.** — [EMTA, income tax for self-employed persons](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/registration-business/businesses/self-employed-persons/income-tax); [palgakulu.ee](https://www.palgakulu.ee/en/tulumaks/)
- **Planned increases cancelled:** the President promulgated the act cancelling the 2026 increases to the income tax rate and the entrepreneur-account rate. In 2026 income tax stays at 22% and the entrepreneur account at 20%. — [EMTA, "Maksumuudatused 2026"](https://www.emta.ee/uudised/maksumuudatused-2026); [fin.ee, Taxes](https://fin.ee/en/taxes); [fin.ee, "2026. aastal jõustuvad seadusemuudatused"](https://fin.ee/uudised/2026-aastal-joustuvad-seadusemuudatused)
- **Security tax dropped:** the 2% security tax on personal income and on corporate accounting profits was cancelled, and the rise from 22% to 24% was later abandoned. — [International Investment, "Estonia scraps part of planned tax hikes"](https://internationalinvestment.biz/en/business/8855-estonia-scraps-part-of-planned-tax-hikes.html). Another source says the security tax was abolished in June 2025. — [Taxravens](https://taxravens.com/en/blog/estonia-personal-taxation)
  - Conflict: some guides still show 24% or a 2% add-on for 2026 ([Corpenza](https://corpenza.com/en/estonia-tax-rates-2026-tax-guide); [CountryTaxCalc](https://www.countrytaxcalc.com/tax-calculator/estonia/)). They look outdated, since EMTA states 22%.
- **Basic exemption 2026:** €700 a month (€8,400 a year), no longer reduced as income rises; €776 a month for pensioners. It can be applied with only one employer at a time. — [EMTA, "Calculation of basic exemption"](https://www.emta.ee/en/private-client/taxes-and-payment/tax-incentives/calculation-basic-exemption); [ERR, "Maksuküüru kaotamine lükkub aastasse 2026"](https://www.err.ee/1609453196/maksukuuru-kaotamine-lukkub-aastasse-2026)
- **Distributions:** EMTA's 2026 guidance refers to dividends and other equity payments "taxed at the company level at a standard tax rate 22/78". It also mentions dividends from which income tax was withheld at 7%. — [EMTA, "Calculation of basic exemption"](https://www.emta.ee/en/private-client/taxes-and-payment/tax-incentives/calculation-basic-exemption)
- **VAT standard rate:** 24% from 1 July 2025 (details in section 3). — [EMTA, VAT rates PDF](https://www.emta.ee/node/336/chapter/10331/pdf)

### Inferences
- **Practical OÜ model (estimate):** profit kept in the company is not taxed. On distribution the company pays 22/78 of the net dividend, which is 28.2% of the net amount or 22% of the gross. Dividends taxed at company level carry no further personal income tax for a resident individual (standard Estonian model; the details of the 7% withholding regime were not verified).
- There is no salary-versus-dividend optimisation need while the person is fully employed elsewhere. (Judgment.)

### Gaps
- **2027:** I found no source on adopted or scheduled 2027 changes to income tax, CIT or VAT. Check the fin.ee "seadusemuudatused" news before year end.
- **The former reduced 14/86 rate on "regular" dividends** (with 7% withholding for individuals): its abolition date (unverified background: from 1 January 2025) was not confirmed. Check [EMTA, "2025. aasta maksumuudatused"](https://emta.ee/uudised/2025-aasta-maksumuudatused), which the search returned but did not summarise.
- **Social tax rate (33%) and the 2026 minimum monthly base:** not verified in this session.
- **Bank and advance-CIT changes:** the search did not cover them. They are likely irrelevant to a micro OÜ.

## 3. VAT: rates, registration, purchases from India, B2B sales to Lithuania and Latvia, reporting

### Takeaway
The standard rate is 24% from 1 July 2025. Registration becomes compulsory once Estonian-sourced turnover passes **€40,000** in a calendar year. Estonian law expressly counts 0%-rated **B2B services supplied to other-EU taxable persons (KMS § 10(4) p 9)** towards that €40,000. So registration is **not** required from the first euro of Lithuanian sales.

**The trap is on the buying side.** A non-registered Estonian business that receives certain services from a foreign supplier not registered in Estonia must register as a **limited taxable person (piiratud maksukohustuslane, KMS § 21)** from the day it receives the service. It must then pay reverse-charge VAT with **no input deduction**.

**Judgment:** register the OÜ voluntarily as a full VAT payer from the start.

### Cited Findings
- **Standard rate:** "Käibemaksu standardmäär alates 01.07.2025 on 24%." — [EMTA, VAT rates chapter (PDF)](https://www.emta.ee/node/336/chapter/10331/pdf); [EMTA, VAT rates and exempt supply](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/value-added-tax/vat-rates-and-supply-exempt-tax)
- **Threshold:** the obligation arises when calendar-year supply with place of supply in Estonia exceeds €40,000 from the start of the year (KMS § 19(1), referring to § 19¹(3)). Registration applies from the day the threshold is crossed. — [EMTA handbook, registration chapter (PDF)](https://www.emta.ee/node/333/main_chapter/10220/pdf); [Käibemaksuseadus, Riigi Teataja](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/130122025021); [EMTA, Registration as a VAT payer](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/value-added-tax/registration-vat-payer)
- **What counts:** from 1 January 2025 the threshold includes taxable supply of goods and services, including 0% supply, except transfers of fixed assets. — [EMTA handbook, Registration as a VAT payer (EN PDF)](https://www.emta.ee/en/node/333/handbook/4/pdf)
- **Wholly 0% supplies:** there is no registration obligation if all of a person's taxable supply is 0%-rated, **except** intra-Community supply of goods and **services under KMS § 10(4) p 9 supplied to a taxable person or limited taxable person of another Member State**. Those 0% supplies count towards the €40,000 threshold. — [EMTA handbook "Käibemaksukohustuslase registreerimine ja kustutamine" (PDF)](https://www.emta.ee/node/333/handbook/4/pdf); [simplbooks.ee](https://www.simplbooks.ee/blog/millal-ei-pea-km-kohustuslaseks-registreerima-kui-siiski-piirmaar-on-uletatud/)
- **Voluntary registration** before reaching the threshold is possible. — [EMTA, Registration as a VAT payer](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/value-added-tax/registration-vat-payer); [entro.ee](https://entro.ee/kaibemaksukohustuslasena-registreerimine-kellele-ja-millal/)
- **Deregistration** after crossing the threshold happens only if turnover does not exceed the threshold in the following 12 months (KMS § 22(2¹), amended from 1 January 2025). — [EMTA handbook chapter (PDF)](https://www.emta.ee/node/333/main_chapter/10220/pdf)
- **Place of supply, B2B to the EU:** when services are supplied to a taxable person of another Member State, a limited taxable person, or a business customer outside the EU, the general rule applies and the service is taxed in the recipient's country. The Estonian supplier shows 0% on the invoice and declares the supply at 0% on the Estonian VAT return (KMD). — [Ministry of Finance, KMS with commentary, January 2026 (PDF)](https://fin.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2026-01/K%C3%A4ibemaksuseaduse%20kommentaarid%20jaanuar%202026.pdf); [EMTA handbook "Teenuste maksustamine" (PDF)](https://www.emta.ee/node/455/handbook/9/pdf)
- **Invoice timing:** for services whose place of supply is not Estonia under § 10(4) p 9, supplied to a taxable person of another Member State, the invoice may be issued by the 15th of the following month. — [EMTA handbook "Käibemaksuarvestus ja arved" (PDF)](https://www.emta.ee/node/334/handbook/3/pdf) (via search summary)
- **Services bought from abroad by a registered buyer:** the buyer self-assesses (reverse-charges) VAT on its own KMD, and the foreign supplier does not have to register in Estonia. — [EMTA, Registration as a VAT payer](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/value-added-tax/registration-vat-payer); [EMTA, General principles of VAT Act](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/value-added-tax/general-principles-vat-act)
- **Received services do not count towards the €40,000 threshold.** Once registered, there is no exempt threshold for services received. — [EMTA, Registration as a VAT payer](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/value-added-tax/registration-vat-payer)
- **Limited taxable person (KMS § 21(1)):** an Estonian person (or an Estonian permanent establishment) that receives a **service listed in KMS § 10(5)** from a foreign business not registered in Estonia must register as a limited taxable person from the date of receipt. This does not apply to (full) taxable persons or to natural persons not engaged in business. A limited taxable person pays VAT only on reverse-charge acquisitions from foreign businesses and on imports. It **cannot charge VAT or deduct input VAT**. — [EMTA handbook, Registration as a VAT payer (EN PDF, updated January 2025)](https://www.emta.ee/en/node/333/handbook/4/pdf)
- **Procedure:** the application is due within 3 working days of the obligation arising, and early registration is allowed (KMS § 21(4)). — [EMTA "Piiratud maksukohustuslane" (PDF)](https://www.emta.ee/node/333/main_chapter/10252/pdf); [Riigi Teataja form regulation](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/et/akt/734784) (via search summary)
- **2025 SME scheme:** small businesses from other Member States can use Estonia's €40,000 exemption if their EU-wide turnover does not exceed €100,000. — [Government explanatory memorandum to KMS amendments (PDF)](https://www.emta.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2024-06/kms_seletuskiri_valitsus.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Why voluntary full registration is the better path (JUDGMENT; accountant to confirm):**
  - A non-registered OÜ buying web or accessibility work from India risks limited-taxable-person status. It would then pay 24% reverse-charge VAT on every Indian invoice as a pure cost (€600 on €2,500).
  - A fully registered OÜ declares the same reverse-charge VAT and deducts it in the same KMD, so the net cost is zero.
  - Registration gives an EE VAT number that Lithuanian and Latvian clients can check in VIES before applying reverse charge on their side.
  - Supplies to LT/LV VAT-registered companies are then invoiced at 0%/"reverse charge" in Estonia and declared on the KMD and the EC Sales List (VD annex).
- **LT/LV customers that are businesses but not VAT-registered:** under the EU B2B general rule the place of supply still follows the business customer's establishment. The Ministry of Finance commentary covers "taxable persons", "limited taxable persons" and non-EU businesses. How a non-VAT-registered Lithuanian company is treated, and what goes on the invoice, needs accountant confirmation (see Gaps).
- **Turnover arithmetic (estimate):** the €40,000 threshold counts the OÜ's sales (LT/LV B2B services at 0% under § 10(4) p 9 count). A reseller with €3,000–5,000 projects would cross it after about 8–13 projects. Registration would be compulsory within the first year or two anyway.

### Gaps
- **The exact list of services in KMS § 10(5)** that trigger limited-taxable-person registration, and whether **IT, web development and consultancy services from a non-EU (Indian) supplier** fall within it, could not be confirmed: riigiteataja.ee was unreachable and the search budget ran out. Unverified background: the EU-mandated identification rule (VAT Directive Art. 214(1)(d)) covers services received under the general B2B rule. Whether Estonia extends it to third-country suppliers needs confirming.
- **Exact invoice wording** for EU B2B services was not retrieved. Unverified background: VAT Directive Art. 226(11a) requires the words "Reverse charge" (Estonian: "pöördmaksustamine"), plus the customer's VAT number.
- **EC Sales List (VD annex) deadline:** not retrieved. Unverified background: filed with the KMD by the 20th of the following month.
- **Treatment of non-VAT-registered Lithuanian or Latvian business customers** (B2B rule, evidence of business status, whether they appear on the VD) was not retrieved.
- **Whether EMTA requires proof of genuine business activity** for voluntary registration of a newly founded OÜ with no turnover yet was not researched. Unverified background: EMTA may ask for evidence such as a signed contract. This is another reason to sign the first contract before applying.

## 4. India side: GST on exported services, withholding, payment rails

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- **Practical (judgment):** ask Indian suppliers for an invoice showing "export of services, supply under LUT without payment of IGST". Estonian Income Tax Act § 29 withholding (10% for services) applies to non-residents' fees for services **provided in Estonia**. Services performed entirely in India would normally not be subject. The Estonia–India double tax treaty was not checked. Verify with EMTA.
- **Indian TDS:** none would normally apply to a foreign payer. Unverified.

## 5. GDPR when offshore (Indian) staff access client websites

### Takeaway
Not researched with sources: the search budget was exhausted. The framework below is unverified background for the report writer to verify.

### Cited Findings
- None. No source was retrieved in this session.

### Inferences
- **Likely role mapping (unverified background plus judgment):** the Lithuanian client is the controller of personal data on its website or CMS (user accounts, form submissions, analytics). The Estonian OÜ is a processor if its work gives access to that data. The Indian supplier is a sub-processor. That points to:
  - an Art. 28 GDPR DPA between client and OÜ, with prior written authorisation for sub-processors and a flow-down of the same obligations (Art. 28(2) and (4));
  - an onward transfer from an EU processor to a non-EU sub-processor, which suggests **SCC Module 3 (processor-to-processor)** under Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914. Module 2 (controller-to-processor) would apply if the client contracted with the Indian supplier directly.
- **India:** unverified background. India has no EU adequacy decision, so a transfer impact assessment under EDPB Recommendations 01/2020 (supplementary measures) is expected.
- **Practical safeguards (judgment; lowest-risk design):**
  - Do accessibility audits on public pages and on staging copies with anonymised or synthetic data wherever possible, so no personal data is transferred and the transfer analysis may not be needed.
  - Where production access is unavoidable: named least-privilege accounts (no shared admin), MFA, time-limited access, IP or VPN restriction, logging and review of admin sessions, no local data downloads, and deletion or return of data at project end.
  - Keep a sub-processor list and get client sign-off.
- **Judgment:** design the default service so Indian staff never need production admin access to personal data. Most WCAG audit work does not require it.

## 6. Contracts and liability (what not to promise, scope, liability caps, IP, back-to-back terms, governing law, insurance)

### Takeaway
Not researched with sources: the search budget was exhausted. The contract points below are judgments, and the FTC accessiBe facts are unverified background.

### Cited Findings
- None. No source was retrieved in this session.

### Inferences
- **Do not promise (judgment):** "guaranteed EAA compliance", "certified compliant", "100% accessible", "lawsuit-proof", or compliance through an overlay or widget.
  - Describe deliverables as an audit or remediation **against a named standard** (WCAG 2.1 AA or WCAG 2.2 AA, and/or EN 301 549 clause 9) on a **defined sample** (listed URLs, templates and user flows; named browsers and assistive technologies; tested as of a date).
  - State that conformance can change with later content or code changes.
- **Client responsibilities (judgment):** content accuracy, alt text for client-supplied media, third-party plugins and embeds (payment, chat, maps), CMS and hosting access, timely feedback, and post-handover changes.
- **Liability (judgment):**
  - Cap total liability at the fees paid under the relevant order, or 12 months' fees.
  - Exclude indirect and consequential loss, lost profit and regulatory fines to the extent law allows.
  - Keep carve-outs as narrow as local law requires (intent or gross negligence usually cannot be excluded).
  - Offer a limited re-test window (for example 30–60 days) instead of an open-ended warranty.
- **IP (judgment):** transfer economic rights in bespoke deliverables on full payment. Keep pre-existing tools, checklists and templates with a licence. Get matching assignments from the Indian supplier so the OÜ can actually pass the rights on.
- **Back-to-back (judgment):** the Indian supplier contract should mirror the client contract on scope, deadlines, confidentiality, DPA and SCC terms, IP assignment, security controls, liability, non-solicit, and audit rights.
- **Governing law for Lithuanian clients (judgment):** Estonian law plus Harju County Court (or arbitration) is defensible for a seller. Clients may insist on Lithuanian law. Under Rome I Art. 3, B2B parties can choose freely, so the main point is enforceability and cost, not validity.
