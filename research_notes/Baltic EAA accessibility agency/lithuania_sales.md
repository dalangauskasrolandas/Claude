*Method note (applies to all sections): research done 30 Oct 2026. Pages could not be opened directly: the session's network allowlist blocked instantly.ai, wcagauditor.com and emailexpert.com, so I stopped fetching. The search budget then ran out before I could do a final check on the disability statistics. Every finding below comes from web-search result summaries of the linked pages. ## 1. Target segments in EAA scope in Lithuania, segment sizes, and which are likely to buy from a small outsourced provider

### Takeaway
B2C e-shops are the only in-scope segment with enough companies (thousands), reachable decision makers and fixes simple enough to outsource for a small provider. Fintech (117 licensed EMIs and PIs) is a good second segment because compliance officers buy for compliance reasons. Telecom (three operators with about 30% each), banks and passenger transport are concentrated, and most likely handled in-house or by large vendors.

### Cited Findings
**Legal scope (who must comply)**
- Lithuania transposed the EAA as the *Law on Accessibility Requirements for Products and Services* (Lietuvos Respublikos gaminių ir paslaugų prieinamumo reikalavimų įstatymas), No. XIV-1633, adopted 8 Dec 2022. It has applied since 28 June 2025. — [Level Access LT fact sheet (Apr 2025)](https://www.levelaccess.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Lithuania-Digital-Accessibility-Laws.pdf); [AIOPS Group](https://aiopsgroup.com/accessibility-legislation-lithuania/); [SADM implementation guidelines (PDF)](https://socmin.lrv.lt/public/canonical/1749467923/4905/SADM%20Prieinamumo%20istatymo%20gaires.pdf)
- VVTAT (State Consumer Rights Protection Authority) says the digital accessibility requirements have applied since 28 June 2025 and that they apply to medium and large companies. Micro-enterprises are exempt: fewer than 10 employees and annual turnover or balance sheet of no more than €2m. — [VVTAT reminder](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos-ir-pranesimai-ziniasklaidai-377/vvtat-primena-apie-prieinamumo-reikalavimu-isigaliojima/); [VVTAT accessibility page](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/prieinamumo-reikalavimai/bendra-informacija/)
- VVTAT lists the checks it applies: screen-reader use, colour contrast and keyboard-only operation. VVTAT says it will also give businesses methodological support. One commentary notes that detailed technical requirements had not been approved and points to EN 301 549 as the reference standard. — [VVTAT accessibility page](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/prieinamumo-reikalavimai/bendra-informacija/)
- Transitional period: the statute keeps a transition to 28 June 2030 only for products already used to provide a service and for contracts agreed earlier ([Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Lithuania-Digital-Accessibility-Laws.pdf), [WCAG Auditor tracker](https://wcagauditor.com/enforcement/)). One Lithuanian web agency's blog mentions a transition "until 1 July 2026" ([web1o](https://web1o.lt/lt/tinklarastis/svetaines-prieinamumas-neigaliesiems)). **Conflict.** Treat the web1o date as unverified.

**B2C e-commerce (the main segment)**
- Store count: ShopRank tracks about 22.9k Lithuanian online stores and says 3,481 launched in 2025. This is a tracker count, not an official census. — [ShopRank Lithuania](https://shoprank.com/countries/lithuania)
- IBISWorld counts 7,539 businesses in its "E-Commerce in Lithuania" industry, with 7.7% CAGR from 2020 to 2025, and puts industry revenue at €970.0m in 2026. — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/lithuania/industry/e-commerce/200600/)
- 2025 market size estimates differ widely because each source counts different things: ECDB US$1,110m for 2025 ([ECDB](https://ecdb.com/resources/sample-data/market/lt/all)) against Mordor Intelligence US$3.18bn as a 2025 base ([Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/lithuania-ecommerce-market)). **Conflict.** The scopes differ: Mordor likely includes services and B2B.
- 41.3% of Lithuanian enterprises sold online in 2025. — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1019392/share-of-companies-selling-online-lithuania/)
- Eurostat reports that Lithuania and Cyprus had the highest shares of people who bought online 3–5 times in the 2025 survey. — [Eurostat, E-commerce statistics for individuals](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=E-commerce_statistics_for_individuals)
- Pigu.lt and Varle.lt are the largest marketplace channels in Lithuania. — [OECD, Competition market study of online marketplaces in PL/LV/LT (2026), LT executive summary](https://www.oecd.org/lt/publications/elektroniniu-prekyvieciu-konkurencijos-lenkijoje-latvijoje-ir-lietuvoje-rinkos-tyrimas_212d9959-lt/full-report/executive-summary_c960bd8d); [OECD key market features](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2026/05/competition-market-study-of-online-marketplaces-in-poland-latvia-and-lithuania_037073b8/full-report/key-market-features-in-poland-lithuania-and-latvia_84d17924.html)
- A Pigu.lt employee's LinkedIn profile claims more than €100m revenue in 2025. This is self-reported and unaudited. — [LinkedIn profile](https://lt.linkedin.com/in/viktor-taujanski-820395b8)

**Fintech: e-money and payment institutions**
- At the end of 2025, Lithuania had **73 e-money institutions (EMIs) and 44 payment institutions (PIs)**. That is 3 fewer EMIs and 1 more PI than a year earlier. Revenue from e-money and payment services was €426.2m. Bank of Lithuania (LB) says most revenue and transactions go to the largest institutions. — [Bank of Lithuania news release](https://www.lb.lt/lt/naujienos/elektroniniu-pinigu-ir-mokejimo-istaigu-rinka-ir-toliau-pleciasi-taciau-pajamos-koncentruojasi-didziausiu-zaideju-rankose). LB's EMI page says it supervises 72 EMIs (minor discrepancy) — [LB e-money institutions page](https://www.lb.lt/lt/el-pinigu-istaigos). LB published its full 2025 sector overview on 12 Aug 2026 — [LB overview 2025](https://www.lb.lt/lt/leidiniai/elektroniniu-pinigu-ir-mokejimo-istaigu-veiklos-apzvalga-2025-m)
- Invest Lithuania counts 248 fintech companies in 2025, down 12% from 282 in 2024, with about 7,800 employees. — [Invest Lithuania Fintech Overview 2025–2026](https://investlithuania.com/fintech-overview-2026/)

**Telecom**
- RRT's sector report (Aug 2025) shows electronic communications market revenue of €452.22m in 2025. The period may be partial. Telia Lietuva, Bitė Lietuva and Tele2 each held about 29–34% in Q2 2025; the exact operator-to-share mapping is unclear in the summary. — [RRT sector report PDF](https://www.rrt.lt/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Elektroniniu-rysiu-sektoriaus-ataskaita_pdf.pdf)

**Local competitors already selling EAA services (useful for positioning)**
- Lithuanian firms publishing EAA guides or offers include Baltic Amadeus, a large IT firm ([EAA guide](https://www.balticamadeus.com/guides/eaa)), site.lt ([blog](https://site.lt/en/blog/european-accessibility-act)), Belownil ([blog](https://www.belownil.com/en/blog/european-accessibility-act-does-it-apply-to-your-website)), SEOCOLOURS ([service page](https://seocolours.com/svetaines-pritaikomumas-neigaliems/)) and web1o ([blog](https://web1o.lt/lt/tinklarastis/svetaines-prieinamumas-neigaliesiems)).

### Inferences
- **Judgment: rank of segments for a small outsourced provider**
  1. **SME e-shops with 10–249 employees** (WooCommerce, Shopify, PrestaShop, custom). There are many of them, the owner or e-commerce manager can sign alone, the fixes (contrast, labels, focus, alt text, headings) can be outsourced, and redesign plus a care plan is a natural upsell. Main risk: price sensitivity, with cheap local competitors anchoring at €150–800 (see section 7). Disqualify micro-enterprises; a practical screen is 10+ employees on Rekvizitai.
  2. **Smaller consumer-facing EMIs and PIs** (B2C wallets, remittance, card issuers). Compliance officers understand regulatory duties, and many teams are English-speaking. The risk is that they need EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1 AA audits that cover mobile apps and onboarding flows, and they may prefer established vendors. Of the 117, only a subset serves consumers (many are B2B-only); I found no count of that subset.
  3. **Mid-sized consumer services with web booking**, such as regional passenger transport and ticketing, and e-book sellers or publishers. These are in scope, but few in number. I found no counts.
  4. **Telecom and large banks**: deprioritise. Three operators share the market about equally and have in-house or enterprise vendors.
- Judgment: the three largest marketplaces (Pigu, Varle) are unlikely first clients. **Merchants selling through them are not themselves EAA-obligated for the marketplace UI**, so target merchants that run their own stores.
- Judgment: e-shop counts range from about 7.5k (IBISWorld classification) to about 22.9k (ShopRank tracker). After excluding micro-enterprises, the in-scope pool is probably in the low thousands at most. This is an estimate with no source; check it by filtering Rekvizitai or Scoris on NACE 47.91 and 10+ employees.

### Gaps
- No official count of Lithuanian e-shops by size class. No Lithuanian e-commerce association member list was found: Ecommerce Europe's Lithuania page shows no national association ([Ecommerce Europe LT](https://ecommerce-europe.eu/research-figure/lithuania/)).
- No count of consumer-facing EMIs and PIs, small ISPs, passenger-transport ticketing operators or e-book publishers.
- I could not attribute Varle.lt's 2024–2025 revenue to a verifiable source.
- The exact Lithuanian share of individuals who bought online in 2025 was not retrieved.

## 2. Ideal company size, decision makers, and how to find them

### Takeaway
Target companies with 10–249 employees (non-micro) that run their own e-shop or consumer app. The likely signer is the owner or CEO at smaller firms and the e-commerce or marketing head at larger ones, with compliance officers in fintech. Rekvizitai or Scoris give the company lists, LinkedIn gives the people, and Apollo/Hunter coverage in Lithuania needs testing.

### Cited Findings
- The legal cut-off is non-micro: 10 or more employees, or above €2m turnover or balance sheet. — [VVTAT](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos-ir-pranesimai-ziniasklaidai-377/vvtat-primena-apie-prieinamumo-reikalavimu-isigaliojima/)
- Scoris.eu (a Lithuanian company database) filters Lithuanian companies by NACE industry, region, revenue, employees and contact availability, and exports results at 1 credit per company. Scoris profiles show employee counts per company, e.g. Rekvizitai, UAB, 17 employees in 2026. — [Scoris catalog LT](https://scoris.eu/catalog/lt/); [Scoris profile of Rekvizitai UAB](https://scoris.lt/en/imone/303341878)
- Rekvizitai.lt (Lithuanian company directory) has a Bitrix24 integration for single-company lookups. Its bulk-export and filter features were not confirmed. — [Bitrix24 app listing](https://www.bitrix24.com/apps/app/dreamsite_inc.rekvizitai_vz_lt/)
- Apollo's data coverage outside the US is described as weaker. A competitor-sponsored page says Apollo's direct-dial accuracy drops significantly for UK, German and Nordic contacts. No Lithuania-specific figures were found. — [Salesforge Apollo review](https://www.salesforge.ai/jp/blog/apollo-io-review); [Dealfront comparison (competitor, biased)](https://www.dealfront.com/blog/apollo-competitor-comparison)
- Pigu.lt advertises marketplace and e-commerce account-manager roles. These job ads suggest the job titles in use ("e-commerce", "marketplace account manager"). — [CV Bankas ad](https://www.cvbankas.lt/marketplace-account-manager-e-commerce-vilniuje/1-13771579)

### Inferences
- Judgment: decision-maker map (no Lithuanian-specific source found)
  - E-shops with 10–50 staff: **owner / direktorius**. They sign everything above about €500.
  - E-shops with 50–249 staff: **e-komercijos vadovas (head of e-commerce) or marketingo vadovas (marketing manager)**, with the CEO approving. IT or the agency that built the site is an influencer.
  - EMIs/PIs: **compliance officer / MLRO / Head of Legal**, with the CPO or Head of Product as co-decider. Often English-speaking or international teams.
  - Transport and ticketing: digital or IT manager; procurement may apply.
- Judgment: workflow. (1) Pull NACE 47.91 (retail via internet) plus other B2C companies with 10–249 employees from Scoris or Rekvizitai. (2) Check that each has its own store (not marketplace-only) and note the platform (e.g., BuiltWith/Wappalyzer; no source). (3) Run the free automated scan. (4) Find the person on LinkedIn; Sales Navigator's free trial is enough to start. (5) Try Hunter or Apollo for email and verify it, expecting gaps for Lithuanian SMEs. Falling back to the generic info@ / pardavimai@ address is legal for legal-entity B2B since April 2026 (see section 4).
- Judgment: Russian is useful for some Lithuanian e-shop owners and for Vilnius- or Klaipėda-based fintechs with CIS-origin founders. Lead in Lithuanian for LT-owned SMEs and in English for fintech.

### Gaps
- No data on LinkedIn penetration among Lithuanian SME decision makers.
- No measured Apollo or Hunter coverage for Lithuanian domains.
- Rekvizitai's own export pricing and filters were not confirmed.
- No Lithuanian e-commerce association membership directory was found.

## 3. Channels and evidence of effectiveness (outreach, partnerships, associations, events, content, disability organisations)

### Takeaway
No Lithuania-specific channel-effectiveness data exists. The vendor benchmarks show small, personalised outbound campaigns doing best. My judgment is that, with 5–10 hours a week, the best mix is personalised cold email plus LinkedIn to e-shops, using the free scan as the hook, and one or two web-agency partners, which can produce repeat referrals. EcomExpo Vilnius (Oct 2026) has passed. The next live events are 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Smaller cold-email campaigns perform better: under 50 recipients average 5.8% reply, against 2.1% for 500+ recipients (Belkins data, via a secondary summary). — [Martal summary citing Belkins](https://martal.ca/b2b-cold-email-statistics-lb/)
- LinkedIn messages to people already connected get about 12.2% replies, against 7.2% overall. — [Belkins LinkedIn study 2026](https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study)
- **Events:** EcomExpo 2026 ("Baltic Ecommerce Conference & Expo") was a one-day event in Vilnius on 1 Oct 2026 (some listings say 9 Oct) with 600–800 e-commerce professionals from LT, LV and EE, 35+ speakers and 70+ exhibitors. Run annually since 2013, so a 2027 edition is likely but unconfirmed. — [Luminik event page](https://www.luminik.io/events/baltic-ecommerce-conference-expo/); [Global Trade Fairs](https://globaltradefairs.com/tradefairs/event-details/ecomexpo-2026); [Showsbee 2027 listing (TBD)](https://www.showsbee.com/fairs/105314-Ecomexpo-Baltics-2028.html)
- "E. virusas 2026" is a Lithuanian e-commerce marketing conference with online-retail speakers, including Pigu.lt. — [VCC event page](https://www.vcc.lt/lt/renginiai/5/e.-virusas-2026-rinkodaros-konferencija/r191)
- **Associations:** no national e-commerce association is listed with Ecommerce Europe for Lithuania ([Ecommerce Europe LT](https://ecommerce-europe.eu/research-figure/lithuania/)). LPIA (Lietuvos prekybos įmonių asociacija, the Lithuanian Trade Enterprises Association) is the Lithuanian member of EuroCommerce ([EuroCommerce members](https://www.eurocommerce.eu/members/)). The Kaunas Chamber of Commerce publishes member-written articles, including one on direct-marketing law, which is a possible content channel ([chamber.lt article](https://chamber.lt/bendruomene/nariai-informuoja/informacija/ar-juridinio-asmens-el-pasta-galima-naudoti-tiesioginei-rinkodarai/)).
- **Disability organisations:** LASS (Lithuanian Association of the Blind and Visually Impaired) is reported to be a member of LNF (Lithuanian Disability Organisations Forum) and the European Blind Union. This comes from a weak directory source. — [1551.lt directory](https://www.1551.lt/paieska/AT/231/?p=285). Latvian analog: the disability organisation Apeirons invited companies to test their services' accessibility with its "A komanda 2026". — [LV Portāls](https://lvportals.lv/dienaskartiba/390937-apeirons-aicina-uznemumus-parbaudit-pakalpojumu-pieejamibu-kopa-ar-a-komandu-2026)
- **Regulator as content hook:** VVTAT says it gives methodological support to businesses ([VVTAT](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/prieinamumo-reikalavimai/bendra-informacija/)). LRT reported that VVTAT planned routine inspections from autumn 2025 and that guidelines were prepared in April 2025 ([LRT Verslo pozicija](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/verslo-pozicija/692/2643325/dovile-armalyte-isigaliojo-gaminiu-ir-paslaugu-prieinamumo-istatymas-kas-pasikeis)).
- **Partnership signal:** Lithuanian site-maintenance agencies already bundle accessibility monitoring into care plans, e.g. Daisoras: basic plan €150/month, business plan €350/month excl. VAT ([Daisoras](https://daisoras.lt/paslaugos/interneto-svetainiu-prieziura/)). Agencies are therefore both competitors and potential white-label partners.

### Inferences
- **Judgment: channel ranking for 5–10 hours a week**
  1. **Personalised cold email with a scan finding in the first line**, to non-micro e-shops in batches of 20–40 a week. Legal for legal-entity addresses since 22 Apr 2026 (section 4). Cheapest and easiest to scale.
  2. **LinkedIn**: connect first, then send a short value message. Especially for fintech compliance roles, where email addresses are hard to find.
  3. **Web-agency partnerships (white-label or referral)**: agencies building WooCommerce, Shopify or PrestaShop sites without accessibility skills. One agency can bring several clients, but partnerships take weeks to start. Start in week 1 alongside direct outreach.
  4. **Cold calls**: use them selectively to follow up emails already opened. A Lithuanian-speaking caller has an advantage, but benchmark meeting yields are low (section 5).
  5. **Content and webinars in Lithuanian** (e.g., a LinkedIn post series "Prieinamumo įstatymas e. parduotuvėms: 5 dažniausios klaidos" [the accessibility law for e-shops: 5 most common mistakes], or a free 30-minute webinar co-hosted with a partner agency, a law firm or an accountancy network). Slow to pay off, but builds credibility.
  6. **Events**: EcomExpo 2027 is worth attending or even speaking at once confirmed. Too far away to count towards the first 3 clients.
  7. **Disability organisations (LASS, LND, LNF)**: user testing with people with disabilities, plus a joint statement, sets the offer apart from overlay vendors. A Latvian precedent exists (Apeirons). Approach as a paid-testing partner, not for lead generation.
- Judgment: accountants and law firms are plausible referrers because clients ask them "does this law apply to us?". I found no evidence of how well this works in Lithuania.

### Gaps

- No confirmed 2027 dates for Lithuanian e-commerce events.
- No evidence that LNF, LASS or LND offer business accessibility-testing programmes.
- No evidence on how effective Lithuanian Business Confederation (LVK) or chamber events are for lead generation.
- Lithuanian e-commerce association (if any) not identified.

## 4. Legal limits on B2B cold outreach in Lithuania (email, calls, GDPR, VDAI/RRT)

### Takeaway
The legal position changed significantly in 2026. Since **22 April 2026**, amended Article 81 of the Law on Electronic Communications (ERĮ) allows direct-marketing emails to **legal entities without prior consent**, provided every message has a clear, free opt-out and objections are honoured immediately. A consultancy summary says this covers employee work addresses (name.surname@company), but GDPR still applies to those addresses. Natural persons still need prior consent. Older sources saying "B2B needs consent" are now outdated.

### Cited Findings
- VDAI (State Data Protection Inspectorate): "2026 m. balandžio 22 d. įsigaliojo ... ERĮ 81 straipsnio pakeitimas" (the amendment to ERĮ Article 81 took effect on 22 April 2026). Its purpose is to make it easier for businesses to inform legal entities about their goods and services. — [VDAI news: "Pokyčiai: tiesioginė rinkodara juridinių asmenų atžvilgiu"](https://vdai.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/pokyciai-tiesiogine-rinkodara-juridiniu-asmenu-atzvilgiu-mrB/). VDAI's FAQ has a related entry dated 19 May 2026 — [VDAI DUK](https://vdai.lrv.lt/lt/klausimai-duk/)
- Legal basis: Law No. XV-815 of 16 Apr 2026 amended Article 81 of ERĮ No. IX-2135. — [e-TAR consolidated ERĮ editions](https://e-tar.lt/portal/lt/legalActEditions/TAR.82D8168D3049); [Infolex Art. 81](https://www.infolex.lt/ta/723156:str81)
- Since 22 Apr 2026, marketing messages can go to legal entities without consent. Every message must offer a clear, free opt-out, objections must stop sending immediately, and proof of refusals must be kept. The changes "taikomi tiek bendriesiems įmonių kontaktams, tiek darbuotojų darbiniams el. pašto adresams" (apply both to general company contacts and to employees' work emails). This is a consultancy summary; check it against VDAI's FAQ. — [ExpertLab](https://www.expertlab.lt/tiesiogine-rinkodara-juridiniams-asmenims-pareigos-2026)
- **The previous rule (now outdated for legal entities):** VDAI's 2019 FAQ said that sending offers to a published company or employee email needed consent from the company head or the specific employee. — [VDAI DUK PDF 2019](https://vdai.lrv.lt/uploads/vdai/documents/files/DUK%20Tiesiogine%20rinkodara%20(BDAR)%202019-07-31.pdf). A Kaunas Chamber article reports VDAI held a strict line and a court upheld prior consent. — [chamber.lt](https://chamber.lt/bendruomene/nariai-informuoja/informacija/ar-juridinio-asmens-el-pasta-galima-naudoti-tiesioginei-rinkodarai/)
- **GDPR still applies to named work emails:** VDAI-linked guidance treats addresses like vardas.pavarde@imone.lt as personal data. — [VDAI direct-marketing presentation 2022 (PDF)](https://vdai.lrv.lt/uploads/vdai/documents/files/12_%20Tiesiogine%20rinkodara.pdf). The attribution of this quote to this PDF comes from a search summary; verify it.
- **Recent enforcement (before the change):** VDAI upheld a 2026 complaint against Printera, UAB over an unsolicited marketing email (Decision No. 3R-183). It found human error does not excuse a breach of Article 81(1), and the complainant was a legal entity protected as a subscriber. — [TheDPO decision summary](https://thedpo.eu/en/decisions/cfad8f56-c8e4-4963-a79a-95cf0c9cea21)
- **Existing-customer exception:** Article 81(2) allows email marketing of similar products to existing customers who were given an opt-out. — [Privacy Cats, Direct marketing in Baltics](https://privacycats.com/posts/direct-marketing-in-baltics/)
- **Fines:** the Code of Administrative Offences sets €150–580 for individuals and €300–1,150 for managers of legal entities for ERĮ violations. This may be dated. — [Lex Mundi LT privacy guide](https://www.lexmundi.com/guides/data-privacy-guide/jurisdictions/europe/lithuania/); [Multilaw LT guide](https://www.multilaw.com/Multilaw/Multilaw/Data_Protection_Laws_Guide/DataProtection_Guide_Lithuania.aspx)
- VDAI also published guidance on direct marketing in the public sector. — [DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/news/lithuania-vdai-issues-guidance-direct-marketing-public)

### Inferences
- Judgment: **practical compliance checklist for the seller**
  1. Email only legal entities (UAB, AB, MB, VšĮ). **Exclude sole traders** (individuali veikla, verslo liudijimas): they are natural persons and still need prior consent.
  2. Put a clear, free opt-out in every email ("Jei nenorite gauti laiškų, atsakykite 'ne'" — if you don't want emails, reply "no") and keep a suppression list with dates as proof.
  3. For name.surname@ addresses, write a short legitimate-interest assessment, and give GDPR Art. 14 information: link a privacy notice saying where the data came from, e.g. the company website, LinkedIn or Rekvizitai. Honour the right to object.
  4. Send from a real identifiable person and company, with no misleading subject lines.
  5. Keep volumes small and personalised. This is also what performs best (section 5).
- Judgment: **cold calls.** Article 81 rules on electronic direct marketing target email, SMS, fax and automated calling. Live human B2B calls to company numbers are probably governed by GDPR legitimate interest plus the right to object. **This is not confirmed by an official source in this research.** Treat it as a gap and check with a Lithuanian lawyer or VDAI before calling personal mobile numbers.
- Judgment: the seller is based in Tallinn. Lithuanian ERĮ rules are likely to be applied to messages received by Lithuanian legal entities, and Estonian rules may also apply to the sender. Not researched; confirm.
- Judgment: avoid buying scraped "Lithuanian company email databases". VDAI has commented critically on offers to sell legal-entity databases ([teise.pro on VDAI](https://www.teise.pro/index.php/2019/10/28/vdai-apie-siunciamus-pasiulymus-isigyti-juridiniu-asmenu-duomenu-bazes/), 2019, older). Build lists by hand from public company data instead.

### Gaps
- The full text of amended Article 81 and VDAI's 19 May 2026 FAQ entry were not read directly. The exact conditions (e.g., whether name.surname addresses are fully covered, and any required content) need checking on [e-TAR](https://e-tar.lt/portal/lt/legalActEditions/TAR.82D8168D3049) and [VDAI DUK](https://vdai.lrv.lt/lt/klausimai-duk/).
- No RRT statement on B2B outreach was found.
- No official Lithuanian rule on live B2B cold calling or any "do not call" register was found.
- Whether the €300–1,150 fine range is current was not verified.

## 5. Realistic conversion benchmarks (2025–2026) and how far they apply to Lithuania

### Takeaway
Cold email averages about 3.4–4.5% reply and roughly 0.7% meetings, with about 2–3% meetings for top performers. LinkedIn averages 21–28.5% acceptance and about 7–10% replies. Cold calling averages about 5% connect and about 5% meetings per connect. Discovery-to-close for B2B is about 15–25%. No benchmark exists for "free scan → paid". Almost all of these figures come from vendor data dominated by US and high-volume senders.

### Cited Findings
**Cold email**
- Instantly 2026: average reply rate 3.43%, top quartile 5.5%, elite above 10%. 58% of replies come from Step 1 and 42% from Steps 2–4. — [Instantly Cold Email Benchmark Report 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026); [lemlist summary of the 3.43% figure](https://www.lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks)
- Woodpecker (20M+ emails): 3.43% average; 5–10% counts as good and 10%+ as excellent. — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Saleshandy (30.31M emails): 4.5% average reply rate for Jan–Jun 2026. — [Saleshandy](https://www.saleshandy.com/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Meetings booked: 0.7% average, 2–3% for top performers. — [Cleanlist 2026](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-02-18-cold-email-response-rate-statistics). FirstSales gives 0.3–0.8% but its tables are internally inconsistent — [FirstSales](https://firstsales.io/blog/cold-email-benchmarks/)

**LinkedIn**
- Expandi (13.2M requests): average acceptance 28.5%, reply to outbound messages 10.4%. Acceptance varies by industry, e.g. 36.5% in staffing and 19.9% in apparel and fashion. — [Expandi 2026](https://expandi.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmarks-2026/)

- Illustrative funnel: 0.3–0.4% of invitations turn into meetings. — [LinkedHelper](https://www.linkedhelper.com/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmarks)

**Cold calling**
- Gong data (300M+ calls, via secondary aggregators): average connect rate 5.4% of dials (top quartile 13.3%) and 4.6% meetings set per connected call (top quartile 16.7%). That works out to about 400 dials per meeting for an average rep. — [CallingAgency cold-calling statistics](https://callingagency.com/benchmarks-and-resources/cold-calling-statistics/); [DialSheet connect-rate benchmarks](https://www.dialsheet.io/resources/cold-call-connect-rate-benchmarks)
- Cognism's 2026 State of Cold Calling: 2.7% "success rate", up from 2.3%. This is a blended metric and not a clean dial-to-meeting rate. — [CallingAgency, Is cold calling still effective?](https://callingagency.com/blog/is-cold-calling-still-effective/); [Cognism figures summarised by SalesHive](https://saleshive.com/blog/b2b-sales-cold-calling-benchmarks-teams-2025)
- Cold call to meeting about 2.5% on average, 5–8% for top programmes. — [SalesHive B2B lead benchmarks](https://saleshive.com/blog/b2b-lead-benchmarks-digital-marketing-gen)

**Later funnel stages**
- Opportunity-to-close (win rate) about 21% ([SalesHive](https://saleshive.com/blog/b2b-lead-benchmarks-digital-marketing-gen)), or 15–25% ([Grow Conversions](https://grow-conversions.com/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks-by-industry/)). Demo-to-close in SaaS: 15–25% average, 30–50% elite ([SaaSHero, vendor](https://www.saashero.net/strategy/b2b-saas-conversion-agency-benchmarks/)).
- Agency and professional-services websites convert visitors to enquiries at a 2–4% median, 6%+ top quartile ([Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/website-conversion-rate-benchmarks)). Median B2B site conversion is about 2.9% ([Numriq](https://numriq.nl/blog/b2b-conversion-rate-benchmarks)).

### Inferences
- Judgment: how well these apply to Lithuania. The datasets are mostly US and English-language, high-volume, tool-automated campaigns. A Lithuanian-speaking seller sending 20–40 hand-personalised emails a week with a site-specific scan finding should perform **at or above the "small campaign" end (about 5–6% reply)**. Lithuanian SMEs receive far less cold outreach in Lithuanian than US firms receive in English (assumption, no source). Against that, the B2B consent rule was lifted only in April 2026, so norms are still forming, and Lithuanian recipients may react badly to unsolicited email. Use the **mid-point (about 4% reply, about 1% meetings)** for planning.
- Judgment: a personalised "here are 3 issues on *your* checkout" email works like a free audit and probably converts better than a generic pitch. No benchmark quantifies this.
- Judgment: for a free scan delivered on a call, a reasonable planning assumption is 20–30% of discovery calls converting to a paid audit, with first-client pilot pricing. This is an assumption based on the 15–25% win-rate range above, raised because the price is low and the deadline real. **No source found.**

### Gaps

- No Lithuania- or Baltic-specific outreach benchmarks.
- No primary Gong or Cognism reports were read; the figures come through aggregators.
- No data on how often positive replies come from cold email in Lithuanian.

## 6. Messaging that works for accessibility services, Lithuanian pitch facts, and draft templates

### Takeaway
The available evidence favours opening with customer, revenue and quality benefits (including older shoppers), backed by a concrete issue found on the prospect's own site. Compliance and the regulator (VVTAT, fines) should be a short supporting reason, not an alarmist lead. Avoid anything that sounds like an overlay or "one-click compliance". The FTC fined accessiBe $1m over such claims.

### Cited Findings
**Messaging evidence**
- Compliance-only framing ("we need to comply to sell") rests on avoiding risk, and tends to produce checkbox fixes such as toolbar plugins that don't fix underlying issues. — [DBS Website, Accessibility vs compliance](https://www.dbswebsite.com/blog/accessible-vs-compliant-accessibility/)
- E-commerce business cases work best when they start from the size of the opportunity and work back to the investment needed. — [TestParty, "$18 trillion question" (vendor)](https://testparty.ai/blog/the-18-trillion-question-how-to-win-executive-buy-in-for-digital-accessibility)
- Karl Groves argues that each common business-case argument is weak on its own unless backed by real data. — [Karl Groves, Chasing the accessibility business case](https://karlgroves.com/chasing-the-accessibility-business-case-conclusion/)
- Organisations that sustain compliance tied the original fix to measurable outcomes. — [accessibility.chat](https://www.accessibility.chat/articles/the-business-case-isnt-the-enemy-of-community-signal)
- **Overlay warning (differentiator):** the FTC's final order against accessiBe, approved 22 Apr 2025, includes a $1m payment. It bars claims that the automated product can make any site WCAG-compliant without supporting evidence. — [Hudson Cook, FTC targets accessibility overlays](https://www.hudsoncook.com/article/ftc-targets-accessibility-overlays/)

**Facts for a Lithuanian pitch**
- Law in force since 28 June 2025. VVTAT is the market-surveillance authority for e-shops and similar consumer services. — [VVTAT](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos-ir-pranesimai-ziniasklaidai-377/vvtat-primena-apie-prieinamumo-reikalavimu-isigaliojima/); [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/verslo-pozicija/692/2643325/dovile-armalyte-isigaliojo-gaminiu-ir-paslaugu-prieinamumo-istatymas-kas-pasikeis)
- **Fines (statutory ranges):** €500–2,500 for placing a non-compliant product or service on the market, €1,000–6,500 for ignoring a market-surveillance ban, €2,500–15,000 where non-compliance harmed consumer health. A warning may replace a fine for a first breach that is fixed voluntarily. — [WCAG Auditor enforcement tracker](https://wcagauditor.com/enforcement/); consistent with [Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Lithuania-Digital-Accessibility-Laws.pdf) and [AIOPS](https://aiopsgroup.com/accessibility-legislation-lithuania/). A legal commentary also cites fines up to €15,000 and a duty to notify VVTAT of non-compliance within 5 working days — [teise.pro (May 2025)](https://www.teise.pro/index.php/2025/05/13/nauji-reikalavimai-elektroninems-parduotuvems-kada-gresia-baudos/). **Conflict:** AFixt cites €2,000–50,000 for Lithuania, which contradicts the statute as other sources describe it. Treat AFixt as unreliable — [AFixt](https://afixt.com/understanding-the-european-accessibility-act-eaa-ensuring-compliance-and-avoiding-penalties/)
- **Enforcement news:** LRT reported VVTAT planned routine inspections from autumn 2025 ([LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/verslo-pozicija/692/2643325/dovile-armalyte-isigaliojo-gaminiu-ir-paslaugu-prieinamumo-istatymas-kas-pasikeis)). **No published Lithuanian EAA fines or inspection results were found as of Oct 2026.**
- **Ageing population:** 20% of Lithuanians were 65+ in 2024. — [OECD/EC State of Health in the EU, Lithuania Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf). UN-based compilations give about 21.3% for 2026 — [Population Pyramid](https://www.populationpyramid.net/lithuania/2026/)
- **People with disabilities:** 229,578 people with recognised disabilities in 2020 (8.2% of the population). Later counts: about 224.9k at the end of 2023 and more than 231k in a Dec 2025 report. These figures appeared in results from the [Validity Lithuania Disability Benchbook (2025)](https://validity.ngo/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lithuania_Disability-Benchbook_ENG.pdf), the [Lygybė monitoring report](https://lygybe.lt/en/news/monitoring-report-reveals-ongoing-struggles-for-people-with-disabilities) and the [European Disability Forum](https://www.edf-feph.org/blog/fifteen-years-on-progress-and-persistent-gaps-in-disability-rights-in-lithuania/). **Which source gives which number was not confirmed (search budget exhausted); check before quoting.** A safe phrasing is "about 225–230 thousand people, about 8% of the population".
- 29% of working-age persons with disabilities were employed (2025 source). — [same set; attribution unverified]

### Inferences
- **Judgment: message hierarchy** (drawn from the evidence above)
  1. Specific issue on *their* site, e.g. checkout form fields without labels → fewer completed purchases.
  2. Customer reach: about 1 in 5 Lithuanians is 65+, and about 8% have a recognised disability.
  3. Low-stress compliance: the law has applied since June 2025, VVTAT supervises it, and an accessibility statement plus fixes "closes the topic".
  4. A fixed price and a named local contact, set against overlays or "AI widgets" (the FTC accessiBe precedent).
- Judgment: avoid leading with the €15,000 figure. It applies only where non-compliance harmed consumer health. The realistic first-tier range is €500–2,500, often with a warning first, so heavy fear messaging could undermine credibility with experienced owners. Fear messaging fits fintech compliance officers better, framed as "regulatory hygiene".
- Judgment: say "automated scan", not "audit", for the free tier. Automated tools catch only some WCAG failures (well known in the field; no figure sourced here), so present the scan as a starting point.

## 7. Pricing tactics for the first 3 clients

### Takeaway
Lithuanian providers already publish low anchors: €150–400 for an audit, €300–800 for quick fixes, €800–2,500 for a typical audit plus fixes, and €150–350 a month for care plans that include accessibility monitoring. My judgment is that the first three deals should be fixed-price, clearly scoped packages at a pilot discount, exchanged for a case study or testimonial. I found no evidence on case-study-for-discount deals.

### Cited Findings
- Lithuanian price points, attributed from search summaries of provider pages:
  - audit plus priority plan about €150–400; quick fixes (contrast, alt text, focus, headings) €300–800; typical one-off total €800–2,500 plus small recurring maintenance — [web1o](https://web1o.lt/lt/tinklarastis/svetaines-prieinamumas-neigaliesiems)
  - audit "from €300", package with corrections "from €600" — [SEOCOLOURS](https://seocolours.com/svetaines-pritaikomumas-neigaliems/)
  - UX audit budgets usually €400–800 + VAT, depending on scenarios, devices and languages — [uxauditas.lt](https://uxauditas.lt/)
  - care plans including accessibility monitoring and WCAG maintenance: €150/month (basic WordPress) and €350/month (business), excl. VAT — [Daisoras](https://daisoras.lt/paslaugos/interneto-svetainiu-prieziura/)

### Inferences
- **Judgment: suggested pilot pricing (estimate, anchored on the Lithuanian prices above)**
  - *Starter (audit + accessibility statement)*: list about €690, pilot about €390.
  - *Audit + fixes + statement* for a small e-shop with templated pages: list €1,490–2,490, pilot about €990–1,490, priced by template count (home, category, product, cart, checkout, account, forms), not page count.
  - *Monitoring/care*: €49–150 a month. Bundle with redesign clients as a care plan at €150–350 a month (local anchor).
- Judgment: pilot conditions. (1) A written case study with permission to use the logo. (2) A short testimonial. (3) An introduction to one peer. (4) Payment of 50% up front. Cap the pilot offer at "first 3 clients" so the scarcity is honest.
- Judgment: price fixes by templates and components, which a WooCommerce or Shopify theme reuses. This keeps outsourced delivery predictable and protects the margin. Get the outsourced delivery partner's cost per template agreed before quoting.
- Judgment: don't compete with €150 audits on price. Compete on "audit + fixes done + statement + VVTAT-ready documentation" as one outcome.

## 8. Funnel math example sized for 5–7 hours a week (ESTIMATE)

### Takeaway
Estimate: about 12–14 weeks of steady outreach, at roughly 35 emails, 25 LinkedIn invites and 30 dials a week plus two agency approaches, should produce about 8–15 discovery calls. At a 15–25% close rate that is about 2–4 paid clients, so 3 clients in about one quarter is realistic. It is not guaranteed; agency partnerships and warm referrals are the upside.

### Cited Findings (inputs used)
- Cold email reply rate: 3.43% average, 5.5% top quartile ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)); 5.8% for campaigns under 50 recipients ([Martal citing Belkins](https://martal.ca/b2b-cold-email-statistics-lb/)). Meetings: 0.7% average, 2–3% top ([Cleanlist](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-02-18-cold-email-response-rate-statistics))
- LinkedIn: acceptance 20.75–28.5% ([HeyReach](https://www.heyreach.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmark-report-2026), [Expandi](https://expandi.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmarks-2026/)); meetings 0.3–0.4% per invitation ([LinkedHelper](https://www.linkedhelper.com/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmarks))
- Calls: 5.4% connect, 4.6% meetings per connect (average), 13.3% / 16.7% (top quartile) ([CallingAgency, citing Gong](https://callingagency.com/benchmarks-and-resources/cold-calling-statistics/))
- Close: 15–25% opportunity-to-close ([Grow Conversions](https://grow-conversions.com/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks-by-industry/); [SalesHive](https://saleshive.com/blog/b2b-lead-benchmarks-digital-marketing-gen))

### Inferences
**ESTIMATE: 12-week funnel (my calculation from the benchmarks above; not a forecast)**

| Channel (weekly volume) | 12-week volume | Conservative | Realistic (small, personalised campaigns) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personalised cold email (35 new companies a week, 2–3 touches each) | 420 companies | 0.7% → about 3 calls | 2% → about 8 calls |
| LinkedIn invites (25 a week) | 300 invites | 0.3% → about 1 call | 0.4–1% → 1–3 calls |
| Cold calls to companies already emailed (30 dials a week) | 360 dials | 5.4% × 4.6% → about 1 call | about 8% connect × 10% → about 3 calls |
| Web-agency partners (2 approaches a week) | 24 agencies | 1 partner, 0–1 referred calls | 2 partners, 1–3 referred calls |
| **Discovery calls (total)** | | **about 5–6** | **about 13–17** |
| Close rate | | 15% | 25% |
| **Paid clients** | | **about 1** | **about 3–4** |

- Hours per week (estimate): list building and scan triage 1.5 h (scans automated; about 20 sites checked); writing personalised emails 2 h (35 × about 3–4 min); LinkedIn 0.5 h; calls 1 h (30 dials); discovery calls and proposals 1–1.5 h (about 1 call a week on average); partner outreach 0.5 h. **Total about 6.5–7 h a week.**
- Judgment: the conservative case gives about 1 client per quarter, so reaching 3 depends on (a) top-quartile personalisation (the Lithuanian-language, site-specific scan hook), (b) at least one agency partner, and (c) using the seller's own B2B network for warm intros. If none of this shows by week 6 (fewer than 4 discovery calls booked), shift hours from LinkedIn and calls into partner outreach.
- Judgment: leading indicators to track weekly: emails sent, replies, positive replies, scans delivered, calls booked, proposals sent. Expect the first paid client around weeks 4–8, given time to reply, a call, a proposal and a decision. This is an estimate; no Lithuanian sales-cycle data was found.
- Judgment: Tallinn and Vilnius share a time zone, so call windows are not a constraint.
