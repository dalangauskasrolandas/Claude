# Narrow the Baltic EAA plan or drop it

- **Verdict: no, not as designed.** At Baltic prices, two service lines in three countries, delivered by Indian white-label suppliers, don't pay within 5–7 h/week. A stripped-down, EAA-only test in Lithuania is worth 90 days and about €500 in cash **[Judgment]**.
- **Demand is real but narrow.** Only non-micro firms that sell to consumers online, plus banks, telecoms, transport and e-books, must comply. Brochure sites and B2B-only shops fall outside the law ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj)). In Lithuania the pool of firms in scope is probably in the low thousands **[Estimate]**.
- **Enforcement is quiet.** No EAA fine, order or named case was found in LT, LV or EE up to Oct 2026. Maximum fines are €15k in Lithuania ([PwC Lithuania](https://www.pwc.com/lt/lt/apie-mus/naujienos/prieinamumo-reikalavimu-igyvendinimas-versle.html)), about €10k in Latvia and €20k in Estonia, and Lithuania can issue a warning first.
- **Margins are thin.** A credible audit plus fixes plus a Lithuanian accessibility statement costs you about €500–1,400 through vetted freelancers, or about €3,150 through an Indian firm **[Estimate]**. Lithuanian providers already advertise €150–400 audits and €800–2,500 for the full job ([web1o](https://web1o.lt/lt/tinklarastis/svetaines-prieinamumas-neigaliesiems)).
- **Recurring revenue is thinner still.** White-label website care costs $39–99 per site per month ([The White Label Agency](https://thewhitelabelagency.com/wordpress-plans/)), against a Lithuanian price cluster of €35–99. Overlay widgets sell automated "compliance" for about $40–70/month.
- **Your edge is Lithuanian plus B2B selling.** Aim it at Lithuanian e-shops with 10–249 staff and at consumer-facing payment/e-money firms. Use personal emails built around a scan of their site; these have been legal to companies without consent since 22 Apr 2026 ([VDAI](https://vdai.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/pokyciai-tiesiogine-rinkodara-juridiniu-asmenu-atzvilgiu-mrB/)).
- **Change the offer.** Drop websites, care plans, Latvia and Estonia for now. Sell an audit plus statement priced by number of page templates, fixes as a quoted block of hours, and monitoring as an add-on. Never sell overlays or "guaranteed compliance".
- **Setup.** Found the OÜ (€265, about one day) when the first client agrees in writing, before you issue any invoice. Register for VAT voluntarily so Indian invoices don't carry 24% VAT you can't reclaim ([EMTA](https://www.emta.ee/en/node/333/handbook/4/pdf)).
- **Stop at week 13** unless you have at least 2 paying clients, each at ≥€500 gross margin, on ≤7 h/week. The fallback with better evidence is referral-selling NIS2 cybersecurity work: about 6,500 entities in scope in Estonia, and a €5,000 roadmap grant paid at 100% ([KPMG Estonia](https://kpmg.com/ee/et/insights/2026/08/alates-31--augustist-2026-saab-esitada-taotluse-kueberturbe-tase.html)).
- **Evidence limits.** The researchers could read only search-result summaries, never the full pages. Labels used throughout: **[Estimate]** = my calculation from cited inputs; **[Judgment]** = opinion; **[Verify]** = secondary, conflicting or old figure; **[Unsourced background]** = not verified in this research; **No data found** = searched, nothing usable.

## 1. Market: the EAA binds a narrow slice of SMEs, and nobody has enforced it yet

**Answer:** In all three countries the EAA applies only to non-micro businesses that offer listed consumer services, in practice mostly business-to-consumer (B2C) e-commerce. No Baltic authority had published an EAA fine or named case by October 2026. **Key reason:** the micro-enterprise exemption and the consumer-contract test exclude most SMEs and most websites. **Next action:** target only non-micro B2C e-shops and consumer-finance firms. Never pitch the EAA to owners of brochure sites.

### Who must comply, and who is exempt

For websites and apps, the Directive covers only listed consumer services:

- e-commerce
- consumer banking
- e-books
- electronic communications
- access to audiovisual media
- the digital parts of air, bus, rail and waterborne passenger transport

Source: [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj). "E-commerce services" means a service provided at a distance, electronically, at a consumer's individual request, **with a view to concluding a consumer contract**. Commentators read these conditions as all required at once ([accessible.org](https://accessible.org/eaa-ecommerce-services-requirements/)). Secondary guides therefore treat purely informational sites and B2B-only shops as out of scope; one of them is a Lithuanian agency ([Going Stellar](https://goingstellar.ro/research/european-accessibility-act-online-stores/); [OPA Reklama](https://www.opareklama.lt/svetaines-prieinamumo-reikalavimai-eaa/)). No official Commission or Baltic guidance confirming this was found **[Verify]**. Some cases are grey zones, and how to treat them is **[Judgment]**:

- quote-request forms
- checkout redirected to a third party
- booking without online payment
- mixed B2B/B2C shops

Practical consequence: **you cannot honestly sell a new brochure website on EAA grounds.** Telling a dentist or manufacturer that the law obliges them to fix their site would be misleading **[Judgment]**.

**Micro-enterprise exemption.** Service providers with fewer than 10 employees and annual turnover or balance sheet ≤€2m are exempt. Product makers are not exempt. Sources: [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj); [PTAC](https://www.ptac.gov.lv/lv/pieklustamibas-prasibas) (LV); [PwC Lithuania](https://www.pwc.com/lt/lt/apie-mus/naujienos/prieinamumo-reikalavimu-igyvendinimas-versle.html); [VVTAT](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/prieinamumo-reikalavimai/bendra-informacija/). Linked and partner enterprises count towards the thresholds ([belownil](https://www.belownil.com/en/blog/european-accessibility-act-does-it-apply-to-your-website)).

Two national wording points:

- **Estonia** says turnover "*ega*" balance sheet may exceed €2m. That reads as *both* must stay under €2m, which is possibly narrower than the Directive **[Judgment; check with an Estonian lawyer]** ([Riigi Teataja](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/115062022001)).
- **Lithuania:** VVTAT says the duty applies to "medium and large" companies. This is probably shorthand, and small firms with 10–49 staff appear to be in scope **[Judgment]** ([VVTAT reminder](https://vvtat.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos-ir-pranesimai-ziniasklaidai-377/vvtat-primena-apie-prieinamumo-reikalavimu-isigaliojima/)).

An e-shop with fewer than 10 staff but more than €2m turnover is in scope, and is an attractive prospect **[Judgment]**.

**Other rules.**

- A firm can claim compliance would be a "disproportionate burden", but the claim must be documented. Non-micro firms must also inform the authority.
- Service contracts signed before 28 June 2025 may run on, but for no more than 5 years ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj)).
- One Lithuanian agency mentions a transition period "until 1 July 2026", which conflicts with this **[Verify]** ([web1o](https://web1o.lt/lt/tinklarastis/svetaines-prieinamumas-neigaliesiems)).
- A Lithuanian commentary mentions a duty to notify VVTAT of non-compliance within 5 working days **[Verify]** ([teise.pro](https://www.teise.pro/index.php/2025/05/13/nauji-reikalavimai-elektroninems-parduotuvems-kada-gresia-baudos/)).
- The working technical standard is EN 301 549 V3.2.1, which Estonia's TTJA names explicitly ([TTJA](https://ttja.ee/eraklient/tarbija-oigused/kaubandus-teenused/digiligipaasetavuse-tagamine)). It maps to WCAG 2.1 AA for web content **[Unsourced background]**.
- Service providers must publish how the service meets the requirements (Directive Art. 13 and Annex V) ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj)). This is your "accessibility statement" deliverable.

**Lithuania changed its law on 1 April 2026.** Amendment XV-760 rewrote Articles 1–3, 6, 7, 14, 16–20, 26 and 29 and Annex 1, and some provisions apply only from 28 June 2027 ([e-TAR XV-760](https://www.e-tar.lt/portal/lt/legalAct/25be17201ed811f1a552c76556910e9c)). The ministry issued updated guidelines ([SADM](https://socmin.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/socialines-apsaugos-ir-darbo-ministerija-pristato-naujas-gaires-del-gaminiu-ir-paslaugu-prieinamumo/)). The content of the changes was not retrieved, so **read the consolidated law before you write any sales copy [Verify]**.

### Enforcement so far: none in the Baltics, early cases elsewhere

**Lithuania.**

- In mid-2025 VVTAT was planning inspections and had received no complaints ([LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/verslo-pozicija/692/2643325/dovile-armalyte-isigaliojo-gaminiu-ir-paslaugu-prieinamumo-istatymas-kas-pasikeis)).
- In H1 2026 it ran more than 1,000 market inspections, 146 of them in e-commerce. All were about product safety, and none were reported on accessibility ([Temidy](https://www.temidy.lt/instituciju-naujienos/12285-vvtat-pusmecio-rezultatai-36-pavojingi-gaminiai-ir-daugiau-nei-1-000-rinkos-patikrinimu)).

**Latvia** is the most active:

- PTAC reminded traders of the rules in Dec 2025 ([bb.lv](https://lat.bb.lv/raksts/bizness/2025/12/03/ptac-atgadina-komersantiem-par-pieklustamibu-un-vienlidzigam-iespejam-klientiem-16730)).
- It made e-commerce and insurance websites a 2026 surveillance priority ([PTAC](https://www.ptac.gov.lv/lv/ptac-prioritates)).
- On 14 Sep 2026 it launched a campaign with a free self-check site for e-shops ([LSM](https://www.lsm.lv/raksts/zinas/latvija/14.09.2026-internetveikaliem-izstrada-riku-digitalas-pieklustamibas-uzlabosanai.a663165/)).
- It ran developer webinars on 30 Sep and 14 Oct 2026 ([PTAC](https://www.ptac.gov.lv/lv/jaunums/ptac-aicina-e-komercijas-uznemumus-nodrosinat-digitalo-pakalpojumu-pieklustamibu)).
- No PTAC accessibility decisions were found.

**Estonia:** no private-sector EAA orders were found.

Elsewhere in the EU, all from secondary vendor or legal blogs **[Verify]**:

- **France:** a Caen court ordered Carrefour on 4 June 2026 to make its shop and app fully accessible within six months, with a **€500/day** penalty. The court reportedly rejected a 71% score on France's RGAA accessibility standard as a defence ([li.solutions](https://li.solutions/blog/eaa-enforcement-2026/); [Atyantik](https://atyantik.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-enforcement/)). Disability groups had sued Auchan, Carrefour, E.Leclerc and Picard in Nov 2025 ([Eye-Able](https://eye-able.com/en/blog/supermarket-accessibility-lawsuit-france-2025)).
- **Netherlands:** the regulator ACM found that 61% of the largest webshops tested could not complete an order with assistive technology ([Atyantik](https://atyantik.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-enforcement/)).
- **Sweden:** the regulator PTS had 28 cases open by Mar 2026 ([Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/eaa-compliance-in-2026-how-enforcement-has-evolved-and-what-to-expect-next/)).
- **Germany:** the market-surveillance body MLBF adopted its strategy on 29 Jan 2026, with reported fines of up to €100,000 ([ITMR](https://itmr-legal.de/blog/bfsg)). Private warning letters demand about €2,700, but their legal basis is contested ([die-mainagentur](https://die-mainagentur.de/blog/bfsg-2026-zweite-abmahnwelle/)).
- Trackers report **no confirmed EAA fine anywhere in the EU** ([Complyloft](https://www.complyloft.com/accessibility/eaa/enforcement)).

**So what [Judgment]:** low Baltic maximum fines, warnings first, and free self-check tools all tell small firms there is no rush. Lead with customer reach and "close the topic cheaply", not with fear.

## 4. Delivery: you can manage quality without coding, but not cheaply

**Answer:** Use five controls:

1. Pay for a test on a page whose problems are already documented.
2. Disqualify on red flags.
3. Pay by milestone, with a free re-test included.
4. Run your own 30–60 minute checks.
5. Keep strict access hygiene.

**Key reason:** automated tools catch only a minority of real barriers, so the expensive, manual half of the work is invisible to you unless you test for it. **Next action:** shortlist 5–8 suppliers, pay 2–3 of them for an audit of the UK government's 142-barrier test page, and keep the runner-up as backup.

### Why manual testing is the whole game

- On a UK government test page with **142 deliberately planted barriers**, the best of 13 tools found 40% and the worst 13%. Counting warnings that need human review, the best reached 50% ([GDS](https://alphagov.github.io/accessibility-tool-audit/)).
- The UK DWP manual puts the best tools at about 30–40% ([DWP](https://accessibility-manual.dwp.gov.uk/tools-and-resources/automated-accessibility-testing)).
- The vendor Deque claims 57% when counted by number of issues ([Deque](https://www.deque.com/blog/automated-testing-study-identifies-57-percent-of-digital-accessibility-issues)) **[Verify: 2021 vendor study]**.

Almost every prospect will "fail" your free scan. 95.9% of the top million home pages had detectable failures in 2026, with 56.1 errors on average. The commonest were low contrast (83.9%), missing alt text (53.1%) and missing form labels (51.0%), and six error types make up 96% of the total ([Priority Pixels on WebAIM Million](https://prioritypixels.co.uk/insights/web-accessibility-is-getting-worse-according-to-the-2026-webaim-million-report/)) **[Verify: secondary]**. WordPress home pages averaged 52.8 errors ([Geekflare](https://geekflare.com/guides/best-wordpress-accessibility-plugins/)).

This explains both why first-pass fixes are cheap and why the hard remainder (keyboard traps, custom widgets, checkout) is not **[Judgment]**.

### Testing, red flags and contract terms

**Paid test.** Give every candidate the same job: the GDS 142-barrier page plus 3–5 pages of a real Lithuanian e-shop. A paid test runs about $100–300 per candidate **[Estimate]**. Score:

- the share of known barriers found
- keyboard and screen-reader findings
- clarity of the fix instructions
- false positives

Pass mark: **≥60% of the 142 barriers [Judgment]**. Tools alone reach 13–40%, so anything clearly above that shows real manual work.

**Disqualify on any one of these:**

- Proposes an overlay, or promises "100% compliant" or a certificate. Some cheap scanners sell "time-stamped compliance certificates" ([Postmake](https://postmake.io/tools/accessibilitychecker-org)). The US FTC made accessiBe pay $1M and banned its claims that a widget makes sites WCAG-compliant ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-approves-final-order-requiring-accessibe-pay-1-million)). The European Disability Forum says overlays don't make sites compliant with EU law ([EDF](https://www.edf-feph.org/accessibility-overlays-dont-guarantee-compliance-with-european-legislation/)).
- The report lists only automated rule IDs.
- Won't name the testers or show certificates.
- Asks for live admin passwords for an audit.

**Contract checklist [Judgment]:**

- Scope: templates, user flows, WCAG 2.2 AA, browser and screen-reader combinations.
- Issue-list format: success-criterion reference, severity, location, fix advice.
- One free re-test.
- No supplier branding and no contact with your client.
- No poaching of your clients for 12–24 months.
- NDA and IP assignment.
- A GDPR data-processing clause.
- Milestone payments.
- Free redo of fixes that break within 30–60 days.

On Upwork, add milestones to one contract instead of opening new ones, which avoids repeat contract fees ([Upwork](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/26106318334611-What-is-the-Contract-Initiation-Fee-on-Upwork)).

**Your own QA, 30–60 minutes per site [Judgment]:**

- Re-scan with your own tool.
- Run the W3C "Easy Checks", written for non-specialists ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/preliminary/)).
- Do a keyboard-only walk through menu, search, product, cart, checkout and forms.
- Listen with NVDA + Chrome and with VoiceOver on an iPhone. NVDA is used by 65.6% of screen-reader users, and JAWS+Chrome (24.7%) and NVDA+Chrome (21.3%) are the top pairings ([WebAIM survey](https://webaim.org/projects/screenreadersurvey10/)).
- Confirm that labels and errors read in Lithuanian.

**Known failure modes.**

- Outsourcing complaints centre on communication (21%), missed deadlines (14%) and ignored instructions (12%) ([Oberlo](https://www.oberlo.com/statistics/challenges-of-outsourcing)).
- Supplier reply gaps of 24–48 h drive client churn ([Boundev](https://www.boundev.ai/blog/when-to-switch-white-label-agency-partners)).
- India is 3.5 h ahead of Tallinn in winter and 2.5 h in summer **[Estimate: time-zone arithmetic]**.
- Never share the client's own admin login. Use named least-privilege accounts, staging copies, 2FA, and revoke access at the end **[Judgment]**.

### Legal limits on cold outreach in Lithuania

**What is allowed.** Since **22 April 2026**, amended Article 81 of the Law on Electronic Communications lets you send direct-marketing emails to **legal entities without prior consent**. ### VAT

**Registration:**

- The standard rate has been 24% since 1 July 2025 ([EMTA](https://www.emta.ee/node/336/chapter/10331/pdf)).
- Registration becomes compulsory once calendar-year supplies exceed **€40,000**. 0%-rated B2B services to taxable persons in other EU states (KMS § 10(4) p 9) **count towards that** ([EMTA handbook](https://www.emta.ee/node/333/handbook/4/pdf)).
- Services you receive do not count ([EMTA](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/value-added-tax/registration-vat-payer)). With €3–5k projects you would cross the threshold after about 8–13 projects **[Estimate]**.
- Voluntary registration is allowed ([EMTA](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/value-added-tax/registration-vat-payer)). Once registered, you can deregister only if turnover stays under the threshold for the next 12 months ([EMTA](https://www.emta.ee/node/333/main_chapter/10220/pdf)).

**The trap.** An Estonian person receiving a service listed in KMS § 10(5) from a foreign business not registered in Estonia must register as a **limited taxable person** from the day of receipt. Such a person pays the reverse-charge VAT but **cannot deduct it** ([EMTA handbook EN](https://www.emta.ee/en/node/333/handbook/4/pdf)), and must apply within 3 working days ([EMTA](https://www.emta.ee/node/333/main_chapter/10252/pdf)). Whether web or IT services from India fall under § 10(5) was **not confirmed [Verify with an accountant]**. Full registration removes the question.

**Selling to Lithuanian and Latvian businesses.**

- The service is taxed in the client's country. You invoice at 0% and declare the sale on your Estonian VAT return ([Ministry of Finance commentary](https://fin.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2026-01/K%C3%A4ibemaksuseaduse%20kommentaarid%20jaanuar%202026.pdf)).
- The invoice may be issued by the 15th of the following month ([EMTA](https://www.emta.ee/node/334/handbook/3/pdf)).
- The invoice must say "reverse charge" and carry the client's VAT number, you file an EC sales list by the 20th, and you need no Lithuanian VAT registration **[Unsourced background]**.
- Treatment of Lithuanian or Latvian business clients that are not VAT-registered: **no data found**.

**India side [Unsourced background]:**

- Ask suppliers for "export of services under LUT without IGST" invoices.
- Estonian 10% withholding applies only to services performed in Estonia.
- The Estonia–India tax treaty was not checked.

### GDPR when Indian staff touch client sites [Unsourced background throughout]

**Roles:**

- The client controls the personal data.
- Your OÜ is a processor and needs a GDPR Art. 28 data-processing agreement with the client, including permission for sub-processors.
- The Indian supplier is a sub-processor. That points to Standard Contractual Clauses Module 3 (Decision 2021/914) and a transfer impact assessment, because India has no EU adequacy decision.
- India's own DPDP Rules phase in around 2027.

**The cheap fix is design [Judgment]:**

- Audit public pages and staging copies with synthetic data, so no personal data leaves the EU.
- Allow production access only through named, least-privilege, time-limited, MFA-protected, logged accounts.
- Keep a sub-processor list the client signs off.

Fintech buyers will ask about this first.

### Contracts and liability: what NOT to promise [Judgment, supported by the cited cases]

**Never write:**

- "guaranteed EAA compliance"
- "certified compliant"
- "100% accessible"
- "lawsuit-proof"
- compliance through a widget

The FTC punished exactly that claim pattern ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-approves-final-order-requiring-accessibe-pay-1-million)), and the French court reportedly rejected a partial score as a defence ([Atyantik](https://atyantik.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-enforcement/)). Offshore auditors disclaim the legal outcome, saying they are not lawyers ([Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/jonasfoersterhu/do-a-wcag-accessibility-audit-and-ada-compliance-report)). So you must disclaim it too, or you hold the liability alone.

**Do write:**

- Testing against a named standard (WCAG 2.2 AA / EN 301 549 clause 9) on a listed sample of templates, flows, browsers and screen readers, as of a date.
- Conformance can change with later edits.
- Client responsibilities: content, third-party plugins, payment widgets, later changes.
- Liability capped at the fees paid, excluding indirect loss and regulatory fines where the law allows.
- A 30–60 day re-test window instead of an open-ended warranty.
- IP transfers on full payment.
- Supplier terms that mirror the client contract.

Estonian law with Harju County Court is defensible, and B2B parties may choose the governing law **[Unsourced background]**. Professional-indemnity insurance price: **no data found**.

### Side business while you are employed full-time [Unsourced background]

The Employment Contracts Act imposes duties of loyalty and confidentiality (§ 15) and regulates restraint-of-trade agreements (§§ 23–24). A restraint during employment must be agreed in writing. A post-employment restraint of up to one year generally needs compensation. Whether a non-competing side business needs employer consent was not verified.

**Practical rules [Judgment]:**

- Read your contract first.
- Get written confirmation if anything overlaps with your employer's business.
- Never use your employer's time, laptop, email, CRM, leads or clients.
- Keep a separate phone, email and domain, and log your hours.

The website/care leg in particular creates "site is down" calls during working hours.

## 8. Verdict: no as designed; run one narrow test, and keep a better-evidenced fallback ready

**Answer: no.** The plan as written (EAA services plus WordPress/Shopify builds and care plans, Lithuania → Latvia → Estonia, Indian white-label delivery, 5–10 h/week) is not worth it.

**Key reasons:**

1. **Margins are too thin.** Credible delivery leaves about €90 per order at €1,490 in the typical case, websites leave €200–550, and care plans leave €0–60/month (Section 3).
2. **Demand lacks urgency.** No Baltic enforcement, low fines and free tools (Section 1).
3. **The model fits your time badly.** It puts you in the middle, holding compliance liability for work you cannot verify, with support duties that collide with your job (Sections 4 and 7).

**Steelman [Judgment].** Your cash downside is only about €300–750. Lithuanian-language, scan-led outreach from a strong B2B seller is rare. The monitoring layer is mostly unclaimed locally. Latvia's regulator is pushing and its prices are higher. That justifies a cheap test, not a business plan.

**What to change [Judgment]:**

1. **Cut to one product line.** EAA only: an audit + statement priced by template, fixes quoted afterwards, monitoring as an add-on. Drop website builds and care plans. Refer any rebuild request to a local agency partner for a referral fee.
2. **Cut to one country.** Lithuania only, where your language is the edge. Look at Latvia, which has better prices and the strongest regulator push, only through a Latvian partner and only after the Lithuanian test passes. Leave Estonia aside: buyers there are sophisticated and a non-profit sells expert testing at €45/h ([Pimedate Liit](https://pimedateliit.ee/testimine-ja-noustamine/)).
3. **Narrow the targets** to non-micro B2C e-shops and consumer-facing EMIs/PIs.
4. **Choose suppliers by test result, not price,** and add a named second-opinion or disability-organisation validation step.
5. **Found the OÜ at the first written "yes"**, before invoicing, and register for VAT at once.
6. **Write no compliance guarantees.**
